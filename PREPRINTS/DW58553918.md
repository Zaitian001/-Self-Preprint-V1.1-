## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Optogenetic Revolution and Its Theoretical Gap

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine recognised the discovery of light-gated ion channels (channelrhodopsins) by Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann, and Georg Nagel. Optogenetics now enables millisecond-precision, genetically targeted, bidirectional control of neuronal activity. Applications span neuroscience, cardiology, immunology, and beyond.

However, a fundamental theoretical gap persists. Optogenetics remains largely an **empirical tool**: researchers apply light and observe responses, but lack a unified architecture explaining why specific stimulation patterns produce specific dynamical effects. Key unresolved questions include:

1. **Why do optogenetic stimuli exhibit threshold behavior?** Below a critical intensity, no response occurs; above it, the system transitions abruptly.
2. **Why are responses nonlinear?** Firing rate-intensity curves are non-monotonic, with optimal frequencies rather than monotonic increases.
3. **Why are certain pathways topologically robust?** Some bioelectrical phenomena persist despite perturbations that should disrupt them.

These questions point toward a deeper geometric organisation of physiological systems—one that optogenetics, paradoxically, has been probing without recognising.

We propose that optogenetic control is fundamentally a **geometric operation**: light stimuli alter the local curvature of a physiological state manifold, and the system responds according to the geometric structure of that manifold. This hypothesis has three components:

- **Topological protection**: Stable functional states are preserved by topological invariants (Chern numbers, Euler characteristics) imprinted during development.

Under this hypothesis, optogenetic stimulation is a **controlled geometric perturbation**: light pulses inject a drift perturbation $\delta f^\mu$ that locally modifies curvature, thereby reprogramming the attractor landscape and guiding the system between dynamical states.

## 3. First-Principles Derivation of the Structural Isomorphism

We begin by stating precisely what can, and cannot, be derived. The claim that cosmic curvature and physiological curvature are *numerically identical* ($R_{\text{cosmic}}=R_{\text{physio}}$) is not derivable from first principles and is not asserted here; it would require a bridge law whose independent justification is presently lacking. What *can* be derived is a **structural isomorphism**: both quantities are the same functorial construction applied to two different objects—namely, the curvature invariant ring of a Hessian geometry generated by an extremal-information principle. The derivation below makes this claim exact and enumerates the conditions under which it fails.

### 3.1 Axioms

The derivation rests on three axioms chosen because they are shared by both domains and make no reference to domain-specific ontology (no metric tensor, no action potential, no Lagrangian):

**(A1) States are distributions.** Any physically statable macroscopic condition of a system is equivalent to a family of probability distributions $\{p(x\mid\theta)\}$ over micro-configurations $x$, parametrised by a set of distinguishable control parameters or macroscopic observables $\theta=(\theta^{1},\dots,\theta^{n})$. The state space is the parameter manifold $\mathcal{M}$.

**(A2) Maximum entropy.** Subject to constraints $\{\langle f_{a}\rangle=c_{a}\}$, the realised distribution maximises Shannon entropy $S=-\int p\ln p$, yielding the Gibbs form $p=Z^{-1}\exp(-\sum_{a}\lambda_{a}f_{a})$ with $Z(\lambda)$ the partition function and $c_{a}=-\partial_{\lambda_{a}}\ln Z$.

**(A3) Observational composability.** Coarse-graining and reparametrisation do not alter the informational content of an inference (sufficiency principle): if $T(x)$ is sufficient for $\theta$, then all inferential statements factor through $T$.

Axioms (A1)–(A3) hold for the vacuum state of a quantum field near a causal horizon and for a multi-scale physiological ensemble alike; neither general covariance nor cellular biology enters.

### 3.2 The Metric Is Forced, Not Chosen

From (A2), the entropy and the log-partition function are Legendre dual, and

$$
g_{ab}(\theta)\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial^{2}\ln Z}{\partial\lambda^{a}\partial\lambda^{b}}
=\langle f_{a}f_{b}\rangle-\langle f_{a}\rangle\langle f_{b}\rangle
=-\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial\theta^{a}\partial\theta^{b}}\Big|_{\mathrm{eq}} .
$$

Thus $g=\mathrm{Hess}\,S$. Crucially, this identification is not a modelling convenience. By the Chentsov theorem—the unique characterisation theorem of information geometry—any Riemannian metric on $\mathcal{M}$ invariant under sufficient statistics (Markov embeddings) is, up to a constant factor, the Fisher metric $g$. Hence:

**Theorem 1.** *For any system satisfying (A1)–(A3), the state manifold $(\mathcal{M},g)$ is uniquely determined and $g=\mathrm{Hess}\,S$.*

Consequently, the existence of a metric—and therefore of a Levi-Civita connection, a Riemann tensor, and a scalar curvature $R$—is a consequence of the axioms alone. At this stage the cosmic and physiological cases are not merely analogous; they are instances of the same construction.

**Proposition 2.** *$R$ is the intrinsic invariant of local distinguishability of neighbouring states. $R=0$ iff local measurements can be patched globally without contradiction (integrable, memoryless, Markovian); $R\neq0$ iff inference is path-dependent, i.e. the system carries intrinsic geometric memory.*

This renders the intuition "memory is curvature" a theorem rather than a metaphor, and it explains why curvature is the natural carrier of cross-scale information: it is invariant under the choice of coordinates and of measurement apparatus, depending only on the distribution family itself.

The original scale-invariance hypothesis is replaced here by a derivable statement. Let $\mathcal{R}_{s}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}$ be the coarse-graining operator at scale $s$, and let $\theta^{\ast}$ be a fixed point, $\mathcal{R}_{s}\theta^{\ast}=\theta^{\ast}$. ### 3.9 Conditions Under Which the Derivation Collapses

A architecture's value lies in how it can be overturned. The present derivation fails if any of the following holds:

1. **The sufficiency structure of physiological observables invalidates the Fisher metric** (e.g. the observation operator itself depends on $\theta$), so Theorem 1 loses uniqueness and the construction becomes a convention.

3. **Healthy physiological states are empirically deep in a non-critical regime** (exponential cut-offs, well-defined characteristic scales), so Theorem 3's premise fails and scale invariance returns to being an unproven assumption.

All three are empirically decidable.

$$

where the first term is a gradient flow driving the system toward regions of lower potential, and the second term is a non-conservative flow sustaining oscillations, cycles, and far-from-equilibrium steady states. Healthy states correspond to deep attractor basins of $\Phi$, whereas disease states correspond to abnormally deep, abnormally shallow, abnormally isolated, or topologically misplaced attractor basins.

The metric tensor $g_{\mu\nu}$ need not be prescribed a priori. It can be obtained through three complementary constructions:

1. **Information geometry.** The metric is defined by the Fisher information matrix of the state distribution,
   
   $$
   g_{ab}(z) = \mathbb{E}\left[\partial_a \log p(x|z)\,\partial_b \log p(x|z)\right]. \quad (11)
   $$
   
   This is the unique metric (up to a constant factor) invariant under sufficient statistics, as guaranteed by Chentsov's theorem.

2. **Response geometry.** The metric is estimated from linear response functions or generalised susceptibilities, quantifying how sensitive each state variable is to small external perturbations.

3. **Dynamical geometry.** The metric is constructed from the local Jacobian and noise covariance,
   
   $$
   g_{\mu\nu} \sim \left(\mathrm{Hess}\,\Phi + \text{antisymmetric correction}\right)_{\mu\nu}. \quad (12)
   $$

Under this construction, curvature $R[g]$ becomes a geometric proxy for physiological plasticity rather than a purely mathematical ornament. Regions of positive curvature tend to preserve neighbouring trajectories and stabilise memory-like patterns; regions of negative curvature amplify small perturbations and facilitate state transitions; near-zero or scale-invariant curvature may correspond to critical regimes, scale-free coordination, or cross-scale integrative states.

### 5.2 Optogenetic Perturbation as Vector-Field Editing

The distinctive value of optogenetics lies in its ability to convert "stimulation" from an ambiguous external input into a structured edit of the dynamical vector field. Suppose the system contains $K$ targetable cell types or regulatory modules. The intervened dynamics are then written as

$$
\dot z^\mu = f^\mu(z) + \sum_{k=1}^K u_k(t)\,X_k^\mu(z) + \xi^\mu(t). \quad (13)
$$

Here $u_k(t)$ denotes the stimulus intensity or control signal, and $X_k^\mu(z)$ is the perturbation vector field associated with the $k$-th target. The structure of $X_k^\mu$ is determined by the expression pattern of the optogenetic actuator, cell-type identity, synaptic connectivity, ion selectivity, and downstream network architecture. Blue-light activation of ChR2-like cation channels produces depolarising current injection; yellow-light activation of inhibitory rhodopsins produces hyperpolarisation or synaptic suppression; multi-colour, multi-target stimulation generates linear or nonlinear combinations of perturbation fields.

Within the broader geometric medicine architecture, optogenetics is one high-temporal-precision instantiation of $\delta f^\mu$. Deep brain stimulation, transcranial magnetic stimulation, vagus nerve stimulation, pharmacological modulation, transcriptional reprogramming, and metabolic interventions can all be expressed in the same form, differing primarily in spatial resolution, temporal precision, reversibility, and biological constraints.

Let $\mathcal{A}_H$ denote the set of healthy attractors and $\mathcal{A}_D$ the set of pathological attractors. The reprogramming task is not to instantaneously displace the system into $\mathcal{A}_H$, but to reshape the landscape so that $\mathcal{A}_D$ is shallowed, fragmented, or eliminated, while $\mathcal{A}_H$ acquires sufficient basin depth and appropriate accessibility. Formally, the post-intervention potential $\Phi'$ should satisfy

$$
\Phi'(z) \gg \Phi(z) \quad \text{for } z \in \mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A}_D), \quad (14)

Curvature plays three roles in this reprogramming:

- **Stability indicator.** Curvature changes near attractor basin boundaries reflect the difficulty of state switching.

- **Memory-retention indicator.** Positive-curvature regions correspond to stable patterns resistant to noise or brief perturbations.

Therapeutic intervention can thus be geometrically formulated as redirecting probability flow from $\mathcal{A}_D$ to $\mathcal{A}_H$ along negative-curvature channels, without destabilising the system's global robustness.

0 \leq u_k(t) \leq u_k^{\max}, \quad \dot z^\mu \in \mathcal{S}_{\text{safe}}, \quad \Phi(z(T)) \geq \Phi_{\min}. \quad (19)

Here $\mathcal{R}(z,g,R)$ may be a curvature regulariser constructed from Ricci scalar curvature, sectional curvature, or Ollivier–Ricci curvature on functional or physiological networks. $\mathcal{C}_{\text{stab}}$ may encode attractor basin depth, Lyapunov exponents, or transition-rate constraints. ## 6. Experimental Validation

### 6.1 Curvature-Dependent Threshold Behavior

**OGM prediction**: Dynamical transitions on the physiological state manifold exhibit thresholds determined by local curvature.

**Experimental evidence**:

Teplenin et al. demonstrated that in optogenetically modified monolayers of cardiac cells, ectopic excitation originates at areas of **maximal curvature** of the depolarised region boundary—where stimulating electrotonic currents are **minimal**. This paradoxical finding—that excitation occurs where stimulus is weakest—cannot be explained by standard critical nucleation theory. It requires curvature-dependent instability at the boundary.

More quantitatively, Connolly et al. showed that ventricular endocardial ridges (surfaces of **negative mean curvature**) had a stimulus capture threshold **0.21 ± 0.03 V lower** than endocardial grooves (surfaces of **positive mean curvature**)—a **24% reduction** in threshold, corresponding to **56.2 ± 6.4% of the energy**. This provides direct quantitative evidence that **curvature determines threshold**.

| Parameter | Ridge (negative curvature) | Groove (positive curvature) | Difference |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Capture threshold | Lower | Higher | 0.21 ± 0.03 V |
| Energy required | 56.2 ± 6.4% | 100% | ~44% reduction |
| ERP at minimal stimulus | Shorter | Longer | 7.4 ± 4.2 ms |
| ERP at identical stimulus | Shorter | Longer | 15.8 ± 5.3 ms |

**Conclusion**: Curvature determines threshold—the OGM prediction is directly verified at the physiological scale.

### 6.2 Nonlinear Response

**OGM prediction**: Optogenetic response function follows $\Phi_{AB}(I) \propto (I - I_{\text{th}})^\beta$, with nonlinear exponent $\beta > 1$.

**Experimental evidence**:

Jia et al. demonstrated that optogenetic activation of inhibitory interneurons **cannot suppress but enhances** the frequency and synchronisation of spiking of excitatory pyramidal neurons—a phenomenon called synchronised post-inhibitory rebound (PIR) spiking. This paradoxical nonlinearity arises from complex interactions between hyperpolarisation-activated cation currents and membrane potential. Strong optogenetic stimulation with high spiking frequency contributes to PIR spiking.

In cortical cultures, frequency-optimised optogenetic stimulation revealed that network-level potentiation depends non-monotonically on stimulation frequency, with optimal frequencies rather than monotonic increases.

Temporal precision studies showed that ChroME2s-expressing neurons maintain single-spike resolution up to **80 Hz**, while CoChR-expressing neurons maintain up to **60 Hz**, with multiple spikes emerging beyond 60 ms pulse width due to cumulative depolarisation and plateau potentials.

**Conclusion**: Nonlinear response is a universal feature of optogenetic control—the OGM prediction is phenomenologically confirmed.

### 6.3 Topological Protection

**OGM prediction**: Topologically protected edge states exhibit extreme robustness against local perturbations.

**Experimental evidence**:

Ori et al. demonstrated that gap junction-coupled interfaces between **non-excitable tissues can be electrically excitable**—a phenomenon they termed **topologically stabilised action potentials**. This topological excitability occurs over a **far larger range of ion channel expression levels** than does excitability in the bulk.

The mechanism is geometric: across a gap junction-coupled interface between tissues with different resting potentials, membrane potential must pass through all intermediate values—including regions where it would be unstable in the bulk. This continuity requirement leads to topologically protected excitations at tissue interfaces.

Furthermore, the non-monotonic dependence of optogenetic control efficiency on illumination area persists across molecular, cellular, microstructural, and tissue scales. This multi-scale invariance underscores that the optimal illumination range is **not a model-specific artifact, but an emergent property rooted in the universal physics of nonlinear wave dynamics**.

**Conclusion**: Topological protection mechanism exists in real physiological systems—the OGM prediction has a biological precedent.
