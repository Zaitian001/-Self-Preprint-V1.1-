## Abstract

This paper develops a unified research architecture in which the Modified Einstein Spherical (MES) cosmology architecture describes physical entities as dynamically organized geometric structures within a closed $S^3$ universe. Its central proposition is that **All Things within the physical domain, including but not limited to all particles, may be investigated as Wuxing Attractors and curvature knots** in the MES Universe Web. The architecture combines a dynamical curvature field, curvature-dependent physical scales, a five-mode state space organized by $\mathbb{Z}_5$, and the Zaitian Quantum Power (ZQP) mechanism.

The paper formulates eight postulates. The first seven address the MES Universe Web, curvature knots, Wuxing Attractor structure, ZQP dynamics, large-scale structure, mass hierarchy, and entanglement. The eighth introduces **ZQP-driven geometric spin emergence** as a research principle: spin is investigated as the rotational transformation property of a stable geometric quantum state, rather than identified a priori with classical internal rotation or with a topological winding number alone.

Within the proposed curvature-scaling architecture, the relations $c(R)=c_0(R/R_0)^{1/2}$, $\hbar(R)=\hbar_0(R_0/R)^{1/2}$, and $m(R)=m_0(R_0/R)^{1/2}$ are examined as linked hypotheses. They preserve $\hbar c$ and $mc$ under the specified scaling, while their interpretation as observable spacetime-dependent constants requires a complete operational and relativistic formulation. A curvature-knot mass formula is retained as a structured hypothesis rather than presented as a completed first-principles derivation.

The paper also develops a geometric interpretation of quantum entanglement, introduces a superquantum postulate as an explicitly conjectural extension, and identifies the mathematical conditions needed to investigate proton spin $1/2$ through ZQP-driven geometric dynamics. The model explicitly excludes a sterile neutrino as a physical mode within its proposed five-mode neutrino construction; this is a model choice, not a claim that all possible sterile-neutrino theories have been experimentally ruled out.

Eight postulates, a dimensional-analysis table, and a set of falsifiable research directions are presented. The architecture is a theoretical proposal requiring further mathematical derivation, numerical analysis, and empirical testing; it is not claimed to be experimentally established.

**Keywords:** MES Universe; All Things; Wuxing Attractors; curvature knots; variable light speed; ZQP; geometric spin emergence; quantum entanglement; neutrino physics; superquantum postulate

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The problem of unification

Contemporary physics describes different scales of nature through several highly successful but conceptually distinct architectures. General relativity describes gravitational geometry, the Standard Model describes known elementary particles and their interactions, quantum field theory provides the language of particle dynamics, and cosmology addresses the large-scale evolution of the universe.

These theories are empirically powerful, but several foundational questions remain open. They include the physical origin of particle mass, the relationship between spacetime geometry and quantum properties, the emergence of intrinsic angular momentum, the physical interpretation of entanglement, and the relationship between microscopic and cosmological structures.

The MES cosmology architecture approaches these questions from a geometric starting point. Rather than treating matter as a collection of entities placed on an independently prescribed background, it investigates whether stable matter-like structures can be understood as emergent configurations of a dynamical geometric system.

This is a proposed research program, not a claim that established theories have been displaced.

### 1.2 The central proposition: All Things as Wuxing Attractors

The central proposition of this paper is:

> **All Things within the physical domain, including but not limited to all particles, may be investigated as Wuxing Attractors embedded in the MES Universe Web.**

Here, *All Things* is deliberately broader than the particle sector. The proposed scope includes, but is not limited to, elementary particles, composite particles, nuclei, atoms, fields, macroscopic matter, stars, black holes, cosmic rays, and large-scale cosmic structures.

This statement is a foundational hypothesis of the MES–Wuxing research program. It does not imply that every listed object has already been shown to correspond to a mathematically established Wuxing Attractor. Such an identification requires an explicit state space, a dynamical law, a stability criterion, and observable consequences for each relevant class of systems.

A Wuxing Attractor (WA) is defined here as a candidate persistent nonlinear state organized by five modes associated with $\mathbb{Z}_5$, with its dynamics governed by a restoring mechanism associated with ZQP.

### 1.3 The MES–Wuxing–ZQP architecture

The proposed architecture has four interconnected components:

1. **MES Universe Web:** a closed spatial $S^3$ geometry equipped with a dynamical curvature field.
2. **Curvature knots:** localized or extended geometric configurations proposed as the structural basis of matter-like entities.
3. **Wuxing Attractors:** five-mode nonlinear state structures used to represent persistent and differentiated configurations.
4. **Zaitian Quantum Power:** a proposed universal nonlinear mechanism for restoring, selecting, or stabilizing admissible configurations.

The relationship can be summarized schematically as

$$ \text{MES geometry} \longrightarrow \text{ZQP dynamics} \longrightarrow \text{Wuxing Attractors} \longrightarrow \text{observable physical properties}. \quad (1) $$

Equation (1) is an architectural summary, not a derived dynamical equation.

### 1.4 Logical status of claims

To distinguish foundational assumptions from demonstrated results, this paper uses four categories:

- **Postulate:** a foundational proposition adopted as part of the architecture.
- **Conditional mathematical result:** a conclusion that follows if its stated assumptions hold.
- **Structured hypothesis:** a proposed relationship whose complete derivation or empirical validation remains outstanding.
- **Open problem:** a clearly identified task for future mathematical, numerical, or experimental work.

The distinction is especially important for the mass formula, variable-light-speed interpretation, quantum entanglement, superquantum extension, and proton spin.

## 2. Axiomatic Foundation: Overview of the Eight Postulates

The MES–Wuxing architecture is organized around eight postulates. Postulates 1–7 are ontological: they describe the proposed structure of the physical domain. Postulate 8 is methodological: it establishes a research direction for the geometric-dynamical investigation of spin, rather than a fixed claim about the physical world.

### 2.1 Postulate 1: The MES Universe Web

**Statement.** The universe is modeled within the MES architecture as a closed spatial $S^3$ manifold equipped with a dynamical scalar curvature field $R(\mathbf{x},t)$ and a geometric field $\mathcal{G}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{x},t)$.

The MES Universe Web is defined as

$$ \mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{MES}}:=\left(S^3,R(\mathbf{x},t),\mathcal{G}_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{x},t)\right). \quad (2) $$

The symbol $\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{MES}}$ denotes the proposed geometric structure of the model. It is not identical to the observed cosmic web of galaxies, filaments, walls, and voids, although a relationship between them is hypothesized in Postulate 5.

### 2.2 Postulate 2: All Things as Curvature Structures

**Statement.** Every physical entity within the proposed scope of MES is investigated as a geometric configuration of the MES Universe Web. Particles are a particular subclass of these entities, and may be represented as curvature knots characterized by their geometric structure and ZQP intervention depth.

$$ \mathcal{X}_q\equiv\text{candidate geometric configuration with intervention depth }\mathcal{D}_q. \quad (3) $$

This notation expresses a proposed representation. It does not assert that every physical entity has already been demonstrated to possess a nonzero topological charge or a mathematically knotted field configuration.

### 2.3 Postulate 3: Wuxing Attractor Structure

**Statement.** A Wuxing Attractor is represented by a five-mode state space organized by the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}_5$.

$$ \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{WA}}=\mathrm{span}\,\{|\Phi_{\mathrm{木}}\rangle,|\Phi_{\mathrm{火}}\rangle,|\Phi_{\mathrm{土}}\rangle,|\Phi_{\mathrm{金}}\rangle,|\Phi_{\mathrm{水}}\rangle\}. \quad (4) $$

Let $g\in\mathbb{Z}_5$. Define the generative and destructive cyclic operators as

$$ S(g)=g+1\pmod 5,\qquad K(g)=g+2\pmod 5=S^2(g). \quad (5) $$

The corresponding spectral relation is proposed as

$$ \sigma(K)=\sigma(S^2). \quad (6) $$

If $\sigma$ denotes a spectral mapping that respects operator composition, then $\sigma(S^2)=\{\lambda^2:\lambda\in\sigma(S)\}$ under the usual finite-dimensional spectral mapping theorem. The shorthand $\sigma(K)=\sigma(S)^2$ is therefore understood in this operator-theoretic sense, not as an unqualified equality between arbitrary sets.

The five modes are abstract dynamical modes. Their assignment to physical species requires independent evidence.

### 2.4 Postulate 4: ZQP Universality

**Statement.** The Zaitian Quantum Power (ZQP) is proposed as a universal nonlinear restoring or state-selection mechanism within the MES–Wuxing architecture.

A canonical saturating form is

$$ D_q=\lambda_q\tanh\left(\frac{B_q}{s_q}\right). \quad (7) $$

Here $B_q$ is a dimensionless balance variable, $s_q>0$ is a dimensionless saturation scale, and $\lambda_q$ has the dimensions assigned to $D_q$. If $D_q$ is chosen to be dimensionless, then $\lambda_q$ must also be dimensionless.

The universal feature is the proposed functional form, not the numerical equality of the parameters. Particle- or system-dependent values must be obtained from a specified model or independently measured data.

The use of $\tanh$ is a modeling choice within this paper. Its uniqueness as a physical law is not established merely by its oddness, smoothness, and saturation properties.

### 2.5 Postulate 5: Large-Scale Structure as Curvature Ridges

**Statement.** The observed cosmic web is hypothesized to be related to the large-scale spatial organization of the MES curvature field.

Let $\Pi_{\mathrm{obs}}:\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{MES}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{cosmic}}$ denote a hypothesized observational projection from the MES field variables to observable cosmic-web quantities. The proposed relationship is

$$ \mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{cosmic}}=\Pi_{\mathrm{obs}}\!\left[\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{MES}}\right]+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{obs}}, \quad (8) $$

where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{obs}}$ denotes observational and modeling residuals. The existence and explicit form of $\Pi_{\mathrm{obs}}$ constitute Open Problem 9.

A complete version of this postulate requires a mapping from the MES field variables to observable density fields, gravitational lensing, galaxy distributions, and cosmic expansion observables.

### 2.6 Postulate 6: Mass Hierarchy from Geometric Structure

**Statement.** The hierarchy of particle masses is hypothesized to depend on curvature, geometric length scales, and the intervention depth of the relevant Wuxing Attractor.

A representative structured hypothesis is

$$ m_q(R_q)=\frac{\hbar_0}{c_0 L_0}\left(\frac{R_0}{R_q}\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{D}_q. \quad (9) $$

The expression is dimensionally a mass when $\mathcal{D}_q$ is dimensionless, $L_0$ is a length, and $\hbar_0/(c_0 L_0)$ is used as the mass scale.

Equation (9) is not a first-principles derivation of the observed mass spectrum. The quantities $L_0$, $R_0$, $R_q$, and $\mathcal{D}_q$ must be defined and determined independently before the expression can produce parameter-independent predictions.

### 2.7 Postulate 7: Entanglement as a Geometric Correlation Structure

**Statement.** Quantum entanglement is investigated through the hypothesis that correlated quantum subsystems may be represented by different components or endpoints of a shared geometric structure in the MES Universe Web.

$$ \mathcal{E}_{AB}\rightsquigarrow\text{shared geometric correlation structure}. \quad (10) $$

The symbol $\rightsquigarrow$ denotes a proposed interpretation, not an identity proven by the equation.

This postulate does not by itself derive the Born rule, Bell correlations, measurement statistics, or no-signaling. Those results must be demonstrated by an explicit quantum model.

### 2.8 Postulate 8: ZQP-Driven Geometric Spin Emergence

**Statement.** Within the MES cosmology architecture, spin is postulated to admit a geometric-dynamical origin: ZQP, acting within the underlying geometric state space, may select and stabilize quantum modes whose transformation properties under spatial rotations give rise to quantized intrinsic angular momentum.

Spin is therefore investigated as a property of stable geometric quantum states, rather than identified a priori with classical internal rotation or with a topological winding number alone.

Let $\mathcal{H}_*$ denote the quantum state space associated with a stable MES–ZQP configuration. The emergence of spin requires a well-defined rotational action on $\mathcal{H}_*$, with generators satisfying the appropriate angular-momentum algebra. The spin quantum number $J$ is then determined by the rotational representation of the resulting quantum state.

The proposed causal structure is

$$ \text{MES geometry}\longrightarrow\text{ZQP dynamics}\longrightarrow\text{stable quantum mode}\longrightarrow\text{rotational representation}\longrightarrow J. \quad (11) $$

**Scope and status.** Postulate 8 establishes a research principle for the geometric-dynamical origin of spin. It does not, by itself, prove that the proton has $J=1/2$. A complete derivation must construct the relevant state space, define its rotational generators, establish the quantization conditions, and demonstrate why the proton corresponds to the $J=1/2$ representation.

## 3. The MES Universe Web

### 3.1 Closed spatial geometry

A representative homogeneous metric for a closed spatial geometry is

$$ ds^2=-c(R)^2\,dt^2+a(t)^2\,d\Omega_3^2. \quad (12) $$

Here, $a(t)$ is the scale factor, $d\Omega_3^2$ is the metric on a unit three-sphere, and $c(R)$ denotes the proposed curvature-dependent propagation speed.

Equation (12) is a model ansatz. If $c(R)$ varies in spacetime, a complete relativistic formulation must specify the metric convention, the role of the lapse function, the operational definition of measured light speed, and the relationship between coordinates and physical clocks.

The expression should not be interpreted as a derivation of variable light speed from the topology of $S^3$ alone.

### 3.2 A scalar curvature-field model

For a tractable mathematical investigation, define the dimensionless curvature variable

$$ \widetilde{R}=\frac{R}{R_0}. \quad (13) $$

Since the scalar curvature $R$ and reference curvature $R_0$ both have dimensions of inverse length squared, $\widetilde{R}$ is dimensionless.

A representative effective action is

$$ S[\widetilde{R}]=\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{A}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\widetilde{R}\,\partial_\nu\widetilde{R}-V(\widetilde{R})\right]. \quad (14) $$

The coefficient $A$ supplies the units required for the action under the chosen unit convention. The potential $V$ must have the corresponding action-density dimensions.

For example, an effective potential may be written as

$$ V(\widetilde{R})=\frac{A\mu^2}{2}\widetilde{R}^2+\frac{A\eta}{4}\widetilde{R}^4. \quad (15) $$

Here $\mu$ has dimensions of inverse length and $\eta$ has dimensions of inverse length squared in the displayed normalization. Other normalizations are possible, but they must be specified consistently.

Variation with respect to $\widetilde{R}$ yields a scalar-field equation of the form

$$ A\,\Box\widetilde{R}+\frac{dV}{d\widetilde{R}}=0. \quad (16) $$

For the potential in Equation (15), this becomes

$$ \Box\widetilde{R}+\mu^2\widetilde{R}+\eta\widetilde{R}^3=0. \quad (17) $$

The sign of the kinetic term and the sign convention for $\Box$ depend on the metric signature and action convention. Equation (17) is a representative scalar-field model, not the complete covariant field equation of the MES cosmology architecture.

The MES architecture may contain additional geometric terms. Those terms must be defined and coupled according to the architecture's own field equations. The present scalar model should not be confused with a complete derivation of the MES Universe Equation.

### 3.3 A phenomenological breathing mode

A small oscillatory perturbation of the scale factor may be represented as

$$ a(t)=a_0\left[1+\epsilon\cos(\omega_b t)\right],\qquad |\epsilon|\ll1. \quad (18) $$

This expression describes a phenomenological breathing mode. The associated scalar curvature cannot, in general, be specified by a universal proportionality to the scale factor without choosing the spacetime metric, curvature convention, and field equations.

Accordingly, the curvature oscillation must be calculated from the selected MES solution rather than assumed to follow from Equation (18) alone.

## 4. Curvature-Dependent Physical Scales and Variable Light Speed

### 4.1 Proposed curvature scaling

The MES scaling hypothesis adopted in this version is

$$ c(R)=c_0\left(\frac{R}{R_0}\right)^{1/2}. \quad (19) $$

The associated reduced Planck constant is proposed to scale as

$$ \hbar(R)=\hbar_0\left(\frac{R_0}{R}\right)^{1/2}. \quad (20) $$

The mass scale is proposed to obey

$$ m(R)=m_0\left(\frac{R_0}{R}\right)^{1/2}. \quad (21) $$

These relations require $R/R_0>0$ in the displayed real-valued form. If the intended curvature domain includes zero or negative scalar curvature, an extension of the scaling law must be specified.

Equations (19)–(21) are linked hypotheses within the MES architecture. They are not derived from the existence of a closed $S^3$ manifold alone.

### 4.2 Invariant products under the proposed scaling

Multiplying Equations (19) and (20) gives

$$ \hbar(R)\,c(R)=\hbar_0\,c_0. \quad (22) $$

Likewise, Equations (19) and (21) give

$$ m(R)\,c(R)=m_0\,c_0. \quad (23) $$

These two identities follow algebraically from the specified scaling relations. They do not establish that either $\hbar$ or $c$ varies in a physically measurable way.

A physical interpretation requires a complete theory of clocks, rulers, wave propagation, local measurements, and dimensionless observables. In particular, a variation of dimensional constants cannot be established solely by assigning position-dependent values to their numerical representations.

### 4.3 Propagation time

For a prescribed path $\gamma$ with spatial line element $ds$, the corresponding coordinate propagation time in the proposed model may be expressed as

$$ t_{AB}=\int_{\gamma:A\to B}\frac{ds}{c(R(s))}. \quad (24) $$

Equation (24) is a propagation-time expression under the assumed local-speed rule. It does not independently establish that a signal can exceed the locally measured light speed, violate a causal cone, or transmit information superluminally.

Those conclusions require the complete metric and causal structure of the MES model.

### 4.4 Propagation regimes

The proposed law implies the following algebraic regimes:

| Curvature ratio | Predicted ratio $c/c_0$ | Interpretation within the ansatz |
|---|---:|---|
| $R/R_0>1$ | Greater than 1 | Higher coordinate propagation speed |
| $R/R_0=1$ | Equal to 1 | Reference regime |
| $0<R/R_0<1$ | Between 0 and 1 | Lower coordinate propagation speed |
| $R/R_0\to 0^+$ | Approaches 0 | Singular or limiting regime requiring further analysis |

Numerical assignments of extreme curvature ratios to black-hole interiors, active galactic nuclei, interstellar space, or cosmic voids are not included as established physical values. They require a specified curvature definition, observational constraints, and a consistent relativistic interpretation.

### 4.5 Observational requirements

The variable-light-speed hypothesis must be tested through quantities that can be measured independently of arbitrary unit conventions. Relevant investigations include:

- time-of-flight comparisons between signals from a common source;
- gravitational lensing and propagation delays;
- consistency of electromagnetic and gravitational-wave observations;
- dimensionless atomic and particle observables across environments;
- constraints from local Lorentz invariance and established tests of relativity.

The theory must predict quantitative departures from the relevant baseline models before observational agreement or disagreement can be assessed.

## 5. Wuxing Attractors as Geometric Structures

### 5.1 State representation

A normalized state in the proposed five-mode space may be written as

$$ |\Psi_{\mathrm{WA}}\rangle=\sum_{q\in\{\mathrm{木,火,土,金,水}\}}\alpha_q|\Phi_q\rangle,\qquad\sum_q|\alpha_q|^2=1. \quad (25) $$

The normalization is the standard Hilbert-space condition under the assumption that the five basis states are orthonormal.

The five labels represent abstract modes in this model. Their interpretation as specific particle species or physical interactions is not implied by the notation alone.

### 5.2 Balance functional

A candidate balance functional is

$$ B_{\mathrm{WA}}=\sum_q w_q|\alpha_q|^2. \quad (26) $$

For $B_{\mathrm{WA}}$ to be dimensionless, the weights $w_q$ must be dimensionless. If the functional is intended to represent a physical observable, its relation to measurable quantities must be specified.

The condition $B_{\mathrm{WA}}=0$ defines a balance surface in the state space. It does not automatically establish a dynamical attractor.

### 5.3 Nonlinear Symmetry Anchor Zero

**Definition 1.** A Nonlinear Symmetry Anchor Zero (NSAZ) is a designated reference state or balance surface at which the selected restoring variable vanishes.

Let $\psi(B)$ be a candidate restoring function satisfying

$$ \psi(-B)=-\psi(B),\qquad\psi(0)=0,\qquad\sup_B|\psi(B)|<\infty. \quad (27) $$

A canonical choice is

$$ \psi(B)=\tanh\left(\frac{B}{s_{\mathrm{sat}}}\right),\qquad s_{\mathrm{sat}}>0. \quad (28) $$

Equation (28) is smooth, odd, bounded, and monotonic. These properties make it a useful candidate for nonlinear feedback. They do not prove that $\tanh$ is the unique function permitted by MES or by ZQP.

### 5.4 Attractor conditions

A state $X_*$ is a dynamical attractor only if a specified evolution law has the required stability properties near $X_*$. A general candidate evolution equation is

$$ \frac{dX}{d\tau}=F_{\mathrm{geom}}[X]+F_{\mathrm{ZQP}}[X]+F_{\mathbb{Z}_5}[X]. \quad (29) $$

Equation (29) is an abstract decomposition. Each term requires a definition on the state space, including its domain, units, and regularity conditions.

To demonstrate attraction, one must identify a stationary solution and analyze the linearized dynamics or an appropriate nonlinear stability criterion. A balance condition alone is insufficient.

## 6. Mass as Curvature-Driven Emergence

### 6.1 Structured mass hypothesis

The proposed mass formula is

$$ m_q(R_q)=\frac{\hbar_0}{c_0 L_0}\left(\frac{R_0}{R_q}\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{D}_q. \quad (30) $$

The prefactor $\hbar_0/(c_0 L_0)$ has dimensions of mass.

The dimensions are consistent if:

- $\hbar_0$ has dimensions of action;
- $c_0$ has dimensions of length per time;
- $L_0$ has dimensions of length;
- $R_0/R_q$ is dimensionless;
- $\mathcal{D}_q$ is dimensionless.

Then $\hbar_0/(c_0 L_0)$ has dimensions of mass.

Equation (30) is a **structured hypothesis**. It expresses a proposed relationship between mass, curvature, a characteristic length, and ZQP intervention depth. It is not a first-principles derivation of the observed particle spectrum.

### 6.2 Required derivations

Four open problems remain central to the mass formula.

**Curvature scales (Open Problem 2).** Determine $L_0$ and $R_0$ from the MES field equations or independent observations rather than fitting known particle masses.

**Intervention depth (Open Problem 3).** Derive $\mathcal{D}_q$ from the specified ZQP dynamics.

**Intervention-depth assignment (Open Problem 3).** Explain why different physical entities occupy different intervention-depth regimes.

**Mass spectrum (Open Problem 4).** Produce parameter-independent mass predictions with uncertainty estimates and explicit comparison against measured values.

### 6.3 Illustrative mass hierarchy

| Candidate entity | Proposed role of $\mathcal{D}_q$ | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Neutrinos | Small intervention depth | Requires independent derivation |
| Electron | Intermediate intervention depth | Requires independent derivation |
| Proton | Larger effective intervention depth | Requires independent derivation |
| Black holes | Macroscopic geometric configuration | Requires a separate field and stability treatment |

Numerical values for the intervention depths must not be selected merely to reproduce known masses and then treated as independent predictions. Such a procedure would be parameter fitting, not a first-principles derivation.

### 6.4 Relation to the MES scaling

If the mass scaling in Equation (21) is adopted, the mass ratio between two positive-curvature regimes is

$$ \frac{m(R_2)}{m(R_1)}=\left(\frac{R_1}{R_2}\right)^{1/2}. \quad (31) $$

This is an algebraic consequence of the assumed scaling law. Its physical validity depends on whether the model defines a meaningful operational comparison of masses across the relevant regimes.

## 7. ZQP as a Universal Restoring Mechanism

### 7.1 Coupling function

The ZQP coupling is represented by the common functional form

$$ D_q=\lambda_q\tanh\left(\frac{B_q}{s_q}\right). \quad (32) $$

Here, $B_q$ and $s_q$ are dimensionless. The dimensions of $D_q$ and $\lambda_q$ must match.

The proposed universality is functional: the same nonlinear form is used across different systems. The parameters may differ and must be specified for each application.

### 7.2 Candidate parameter hierarchy

| System | $\lambda_q$ | $s_q$ | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neutrino sector | To be determined | To be determined | Candidate weakly coupled mode |
| Electron | To be determined | To be determined | Candidate charged-lepton mode |
| Proton | To be determined | To be determined | Candidate composite-hadron mode |
| Black hole | To be determined | To be determined | Requires a macroscopic field description |

The table intentionally leaves numerical values unspecified. Arbitrary orders of magnitude would not be physically meaningful without a derivation or a clearly identified fit.

### 7.3 Candidate restoring-rate law

A proposed curvature-dependent rate is

$$ \Gamma_q(R)=\gamma_0\lambda_q\left(\frac{R}{R_0}\right)^{3/2}. \quad (33) $$

For $\Gamma_q$ to have dimensions of inverse time, $\gamma_0\lambda_q$ must have those dimensions if the curvature ratio is dimensionless.

A representative stochastic equation is

$$ \frac{dB_q}{dt}=-\Gamma_q(R)\,\psi(B_q)+\xi_q(t). \quad (34) $$

Here, $\xi_q(t)$ represents a noise term whose statistical properties must be specified.

For small $B_q$, the approximation $\tanh(B_q/s_q)\approx B_q/s_q$ gives

$$ \frac{dB_q}{dt}\approx-\frac{\Gamma_q(R)}{s_q}B_q+\xi_q(t). \quad (35) $$

The deterministic linearized equation has a restoring tendency when $\Gamma_q(R)/s_q>0$. The scaling $\Gamma_q\propto R^{3/2}$, however, remains a structured hypothesis until derived from a specified ZQP field equation or independently tested.

### 7.4 Open quantum dynamics

The broader MES architecture considers curvature-coupled open quantum dynamics. A standard candidate representation for a Markovian reduced density operator is the Lindblad equation

$$ \frac{d\rho}{dt}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H,\rho]+\sum_j\left(L_j\rho L_j^\dagger-\frac{1}{2}\{L_j^\dagger L_j,\rho\}\right). \quad (36) $$

Equation (36) is a standard open-quantum-system form. To use it as an MES prediction, the Hamiltonian $H$, the operators $L_j$, and their dependence on the MES curvature field must be derived or explicitly modeled.

A derivation from microscopic dynamics requires assumptions concerning the system–environment split, coupling strength, bath correlations, and relevant time scales. Curvature dependence alone does not guarantee a Lindblad equation.

## 9. Nucleon Geometry and the Emergence of Spin

### 9.1 Nucleon state representation

A nucleon candidate state in the five-mode architecture may be written as

$$ |N\rangle=\sum_q c_q|\Phi_q\rangle,\qquad\sum_q|c_q|^2=1. \quad (41) $$

### 9.2 Why topology alone does not prove proton spin

For an appropriately defined SU(3)-valued effective field $U:S^3\to SU(3)$, the homotopy relation

$$ \pi_3(SU(3))\cong\mathbb{Z} \quad (42) $$

permits integer-valued topological sectors.

With a suitable orientation and normalization, one may define a winding number

$$ B[U]=\frac{1}{24\pi^2}\int_{S^3}\mathrm{Tr}\,\bigl[(U^{-1}dU)^3\bigr]\in\mathbb{Z}. \quad (43) $$

The exact overall sign depends on conventions for the trace and orientation.

Equations (42) and (43) establish the mathematical possibility of classifying suitable field configurations by an integer topological charge. They do not establish that the proton spin equals one-half, and they do not establish that every SU(3) color-gauge configuration carries the physical baryon number.

The physical interpretation of $B[U]$ depends on the field theory in which $U$ is defined. In a chiral effective theory, topological winding can be related to baryon number under specific conditions. This relationship cannot be transferred automatically to an arbitrary MES geometric field.

### 9.3 ZQP-driven geometric spin emergence

Postulate 8 reverses the question. Rather than presupposing a proton spin of one-half and identifying it with a winding number, it asks whether ZQP-driven geometric dynamics can produce stable quantum states with the appropriate rotational transformation properties.

Let $\mathcal{C}_B$ denote a configuration space restricted to a well-defined topological sector, and let $\gamma_{2\pi}$ represent a path corresponding to a full spatial rotation of a candidate configuration. A complete quantization must determine whether this path is contractible and what phase the corresponding quantum state acquires.

If the model independently establishes

$$ \Psi[\gamma_{2\pi}(U)]=-\Psi[U], \quad (44) $$

then the state transforms with a minus sign under a $2\pi$ rotation. In an ordinary quantum theory with the appropriate rotation representation, this is consistent with half-integer spin.

However, Equation (44) is a condition to be derived from the model's configuration-space topology and quantization rules. It does not follow from the integer winding number alone.

### 9.4 The distinction between half-integer spin and spin one-half

The angular momentum algebra permits

$$ J\in\{\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{5}{2},\ldots\} \quad (45) $$

for half-integer-spin representations.

Consequently, even a successful derivation of the minus sign in Equation (44) would not by itself select $J=1/2$. To establish the proton's specific spin, the theory must additionally derive the relevant energy spectrum, quantum numbers, and state-selection mechanism.

## 10. Quantum Entanglement and the Geometric Correlation Hypothesis

### 10.1 A representative entangled state

This is a standard quantum-state form under the assumption that the two basis states represent the appropriate orthogonal degrees of freedom. The expression does not establish that the subsystems are physically connected by a single geometric knot.

### 10.2 Geometric hypothesis

The proposed MES interpretation is

$$ \text{entanglement}\rightsquigarrow\text{shared geometric correlation structure}. \quad (47) $$

The hypothesis is that the correlated subsystems may be represented as parts or endpoints of a common geometric structure. To turn this into a physical model, the relationship between the geometric state and the quantum density operator must be explicitly defined.

In particular, the model must reproduce quantum measurement probabilities and the correlations observed in Bell-type experiments.

### 10.3 A candidate geometric kernel

A possible positive elliptic operator on $S^3$ can be written as

$$ \hat{A}=-\Delta_{S^3}+\mu_K^2,\qquad \mu_K>0. \quad (48) $$

Here, $-\Delta_{S^3}$ is the nonnegative Laplace–Beltrami operator on the specified spatial geometry, and $\mu_K$ has dimensions of inverse length.

The corresponding Green operator is

$$ \hat{K}=\hat{A}^{-1}. \quad (49) $$

Let the eigenfunctions satisfy

$$ -\Delta_{S^3}\phi_k=\kappa_k^2\phi_k. \quad (50) $$

For an orthonormal basis under the selected volume measure,

$$ \int_{S^3}\phi_k(\mathbf{x})\,\phi_{k'}^*(\mathbf{x})\,dV=\delta_{kk'}. \quad (51) $$

The formal kernel is then

$$ K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}')=\sum_k\frac{\phi_k(\mathbf{x})\,\phi_k^*(\mathbf{x}')}{\kappa_k^2+\mu_K^2}. \quad (52) $$

The dimensions of the kernel depend on the normalization of the eigenfunctions and the measure. The kernel also depends on the chosen metric and boundary conditions.

Equations (48)–(52) define a possible mathematical tool for representing spatial correlations. They do not demonstrate that quantum entanglement is mediated by this kernel, nor that the kernel determines a quantum measurement outcome.

### 10.4 Nonlocal correlation and causality

The idea of "instantaneous activation" is retained only as a geometric hypothesis about nonlocal correlation. It is not asserted to be a physical signal traveling instantaneously between spatially separated systems.

A valid MES account must demonstrate that its predicted measurement statistics reproduce the relevant quantum correlations and satisfy the no-signaling condition. It must also specify how the model's causal structure is related to operationally measured propagation speeds.

**Open Problem 7.** Derive the measurement statistics of the WA architecture, prove the no-signaling condition under clearly specified assumptions, and establish consistency with the causal structure of the MES model.

### 10.5 Relation to Bell inequalities

For the standard CHSH experiment, the local-hidden-variable bound is $2$, while quantum theory predicts a maximum value of $2\sqrt{2}$ for the usual quantum-mechanical correlations under the standard assumptions.

A proposed MES extension must not conflate a mathematical parameter with an experimentally measured CHSH correlation. Any claimed departure from the standard quantum bound requires a complete specification of the observables, probabilities, normalization, and experimental protocol.

## 11. The Superquantum Extension as a Separate Conjecture

The superquantum expression is not counted as an additional foundational postulate in this version. The eight-postulate architecture is preserved, while the proposed extension is formulated separately as a conjectural research hypothesis.

$$ S_{\mathrm{ZQP}}=2\sqrt{2}\left(1+\frac{\lambda_q R}{s_q R_0}\right). \quad (53) $$

**Status of the superquantum expression.** The expression $S_{\mathrm{ZQP}}=2\sqrt{2}(1+\lambda_q R/(s_q R_0))$ is presented only as a **mathematical placeholder** for a possible curvature-dependent correlation. It is not claimed that this expression is dimensionally identical to a CHSH parameter, nor that it violates the Tsirelson bound in any operational sense. A consistent derivation from the MES measurement model remains an open problem.

For the ratio in parentheses to be dimensionless, $\lambda_q$ and $s_q$ must have compatible dimensions, and $R/R_0$ is dimensionless. If $\lambda_q$ and $s_q$ are both dimensionless, the expression is dimensionally consistent.

### 11.2 Mathematical and empirical requirements

If Equation (53) is intended to represent an actual CHSH correlation parameter, it must be reconciled with the definitions of the measured observables and the probability structure of the theory.

In standard quantum theory, a CHSH parameter formed from the usual dichotomic observables is bounded by $2\sqrt{2}$. Therefore, if the correction factor in Equation (53) is positive and nonzero, the expression exceeds the standard quantum bound. Such a result cannot be presented as an ordinary quantum-mechanical prediction without further qualification.

A superquantum interpretation would require, at minimum:

1. a precise definition of the proposed observables;
2. a normalized probability model;
3. explicit predictions for measurement outcomes;
4. a demonstration of no-signaling, or a clearly stated alternative;
5. a causal and relativistic interpretation;
6. a testable distinction from standard quantum theory.

**Open Problem 6.** Derive or revise Equation (53) from a fully specified MES–ZQP measurement model, and determine whether the resulting theory is mathematically consistent, operationally meaningful, and empirically distinguishable from standard quantum mechanics.

The MES architecture is the theoretical starting point of this paper. The purpose of the mathematical sections is not to presuppose that every relation has already been derived, but to identify the equations, definitions, and tests needed to make the architecture precise.

A complete future treatment should integrate the curvature field equations, ZQP dynamics, state-space structure, and observable predictions into a consistent mathematical model.
