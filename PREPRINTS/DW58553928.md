## Abstract

This paper proposes **Creating Gold** as the first exercise of a **new beginning for creating matter**. The target-state construction is organized as a finite, verifiable sequence of operator applications: target encoding, path planning, window transition, condensation, and stabilization. The Creating Gold paradigm is developed within the author's Modified Einstein Spherical (MES) architecture and the `emind` architecture, anchored by the three Gold Truths as foundational MES postulates. The MES curvature-dependent fields are inherited unchanged from DW58553914. The Global Topological Curvature Network (GTCN) is retained as the same-level geometric child of the MES universe curvature web, with its dimension, embedding data, characteristic structure, and channel maps stated as mathematical construction requirements. The paper distinguishes Type 1 formal research architecture, Type 2 mathematical model existence, and Type 3 physical realization. Gold-197 is the first benchmark, not a claim of completed transmutation. The five-channel evidence-state vector records each channel as supported, ruled out within a declared domain, or unresolved. The GTCN and the Bottleneck Loop provide the global geometric and physical-cycle structures through which the construction program is to be investigated. A methodological analogy drawn from the Zhuangzi's parable of Wheelwright Pian clarifies the two-sided constraint on the finite transition window and the necessity of responsive closed-loop control over static open-loop prescriptions.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Why Gold, and Why a New Beginning

Gold-197 is the first benchmark for a broader question. The conventional route to gold—neutron irradiation of mercury-198 followed by electron capture, or high-energy bombardment of bismuth—is physically established and economically costly. This paper does not compete with those routes. It asks whether the creation of a target state can be organized as a **constructive problem** rather than an assembly problem, and whether gold can serve as the first case through which a new beginning for creating matter is exercised.

The distinction is not semantic. An assembly paradigm decomposes a target into constituents and forces the constituents together. Its mathematical structure is a sequence of reaction operators between particle-number sectors. A construction paradigm represents the target as a distinguished state or attractor in a declared state space and asks whether a finite sequence of admissible transformations connects an initial state to that target, with explicit criteria at each step.

The Creating Gold paradigm developed here rests on three methodological propositions:

1. **Creation as construction.** A target state is represented as an attractor in a specified state space; creating it means constructing a finite operator chain from an admissible initial state to the target.
2. **Construction as verification.** Each stage of the chain carries a mathematical definition, a verification obligation, and an explicit failure condition. The Creating Gold paradigm is judged by whether the obligations are met, not by whether the narrative is persuasive.
3. **Anchoring as extension.** Gold-197 is the first anchor. The Creating Gold paradigm transfers to other targets—other elements, other phases, other scales—only by replacing the target encoding and re-evaluating every obligation independently.

### 1.2 Three Gold Truths

The Creating Gold paradigm is anchored by three Gold Truths. They are foundational postulates of the MES cosmological architecture and are self-evident within that architecture. Their wording and status are preserved identically in the sister papers.

> **Gold Truth I (Geometry First).** There are no independently existing physical constants, no abstract pure time, and no isolated biological entities. All physics is geometry. Every observable physical quantity and biological state is a natural emergence of the geometric structure of the MES universe curvature web.

**Formal location.** Gold Truth I is supported by A1 (curvature–mass), A4 (observer-origin geometry), and A6 (non-Hermitian effective drive). Physical constants are not parameters external to the manifold; they are functions of the local curvature scale. Biological entities are not isolated subjects detached from geometry; they are emergent states of specific attractor configurations in the curvature web.

> **Gold Truth II (Time as Rhythm).** "Time is the countable rhythm of Yin–Yang waxing and waning 时间是阴阳消长的可计数节律."

**Formal location.** Time is not an independent background; it is the countable rhythm of Yin–Yang waxing and waning. In the mathematical model, this rhythm is represented by the finite transition interval $\tau_{Ji} > 0$ of A5 and by the countable parameter $\tau$ along which the construction chain is indexed.

> **Gold Truth III (Mother and Child).** "Obtain the mother to know the child; obtain the child to guard the mother 既得其母以知其子, 既知其子复守其母." The MES universe curvature web is the mother; the global topological curvature network is the child; their mutual guarding is guaranteed by the Gauss–Codazzi embedding constraint.

**Formal location.** The mother is the MES universe curvature web (L0). The child is the Global Topological Curvature Network (GTCN, L1). The forward direction—obtain the mother to know the child—is represented by the channel-scale geometric projection. The reverse direction—obtain the child to guard the mother—is represented within the MES architecture by the embedding-compatibility system. For a codimension-one embedding, the Gauss and Codazzi equations may be written, under a declared curvature-sign convention, as

$$

where $K_{ab}$ is the second fundamental form and $\varepsilon=g(n,n)=\pm1$. For higher codimension, the normal-bundle connection and Ricci equation must also be included. Gold Truth III remains the MES foundational postulate; these tensor equations state the geometric compatibility conditions required for its embedding realization. The scalar contraction is not the complete embedding system. The full tensor form is restated with explicit geometric data in Section 5.3.2, where the four-dimensional GTCN is constructed.

These relations are structural hypotheses within the MES architecture. They are not first-principles derivations or experimentally established laws. Their physical use requires a precise definition of $R$, dimensional conventions, and independent empirical constraints.

### 1.4 Two Guiding Scientific Principles

The paper is organized around two principles that must be held simultaneously. Stated in symmetric form:

> **Principle P1 (Against Overclaiming).** A physical target that has not been realized must not be presented as a completed scientific discovery.
>
> **Principle P2 (Against Overrejection).** An architecture that may have mathematical value must not be rejected merely because its physical realization has not been demonstrated.

The symmetry is deliberate. P1 and P2 are not opposing constraints that must be balanced against each other. They oppose the **same** error from two sides: conflating formal constructive specification with physical realization.

- P1 conflates by inflation: it presents a formal construction as a physical achievement.
- P2 conflates by deflation: it treats the absence of physical realization as a refutation of the mathematical architecture.
- The correct response to both is the same: **keep Type 1 and Type 3 distinct**, and state the claim appropriate to each level.

This distinction is operationalized in Section 1.5 through a three-level typology of existence, and it governs every claim in this paper. When the paper asserts that the construction chain is formally specified (Type 1), it does not thereby assert that gold has been produced (Type 3). When the paper observes that Type 3 remains undecided, it does not thereby retract the Type-1 claim. Each level stands or falls on its own criteria.

A corollary follows, which the paper adopts as a methodological rule:

> **Rule of Level-Appropriate Claim.** Every assertion in this paper must identify which level of existence it addresses, and the strength of the assertion must not exceed what that level supports.

This rule is applied explicitly in Section 9 (Boundary of the Claims) and in Appendix A (Open Problems).

### 1.5 Three Levels of Existence

The paper distinguishes three levels, each with its own criteria and its own claim status.

**Type 1 — Formal constructive specification.** A construction chain is specified within an axiom system; every stage has a mathematical definition, a domain, and a verification obligation. Type 1 does not require that a model satisfying the axioms exists, nor that the construction corresponds to a physical process. It requires only that the specification be internally coherent and the obligations be statable.

**Type 2 — Mathematical model existence.** A mathematical model satisfying the axiom set and the construction requirements exists. Type 2 is conditional on the consistency of the axiom set; the absence of a discovered contradiction is not a proof of consistency.

**Type 3 — Physical realization.** The construction corresponds to a realizable physical process, with physically defined degrees of freedom, interactions, initial and boundary conditions, experimentally meaningful observables, and quantitative agreement with established data.

The present paper claims **Type 1**. It notes that **Type 2** is conditional and open. It **suspends Type 3** without asserting or denying it; the suspension is a precise statement that the required environmental and nuclear parameters have not been computed.

## 2. Proposed Axiom Set

The construction architecture is developed within the following proposed axiom set. A1–A6 are structural postulates adopted from the author's MES and `emind` research program. B1 is the cross-scale bridge proposed in this paper. None is presented as an experimentally established law. The consistency of the set is not proved; it is an open formal question (Appendix A.1).

### A1. Curvature–Mass Postulate

A localized state is assigned an inertial mass that is modeled as a function of a local scalar-curvature variable:

$$
m(R) = m_0 \left(\frac{R_0}{R}\right)^{1/2}. \quad (6)
$$

Here $m_0$ and $R_0$ are reference quantities and $R$ is the curvature variable of the MES model. This is a structured postulate within the architecture, not a first-principles derivation or an established mass law. Its physical use requires a precise definition of $R$, dimensional conventions, and independent empirical constraints.

### A2. Topological Condensation Postulate

Selected states of matter may be represented as topological condensates of a dynamical equilibrium quantum manifold. A nonlinear feedback mechanism, referred to in the MES program as Zaitian Quantum Power (ZQP), is proposed as a possible stabilizing ingredient. For the purposes of this paper, A2 is a qualitative structural postulate. A quantitative version must specify the state variable, the evolution equation, the feedback functional, parameter dimensions, and stability conditions. No general theorem of condensation is assumed.

### A3. Landscape-Reprogramming Postulate

A potential or effective-state landscape may be modified by a perturbation operator:

$$
\hat{\mathcal O}_{\mathrm{therapy}}. \quad (7)
$$

The operator's domain, codomain, Hermiticity properties, and coupling to the controlled system must be specified in any concrete implementation. The claimed flattening of saddle regions or deepening of target wells is a model objective, not an established consequence of introducing the symbol.

### A4. Observer-Origin Geometry

An observer-origin orientation is represented by a real skew-symmetric generator $A$,

$$
A^{T} = -A, \quad (8)
$$

with a corresponding rotation:

$$
R(\theta) = \exp(\theta A) \in SO(3). \quad (9)
$$

The generator is a Luoshu-inspired design convention within the author's geometric architecture. It provides an orientation structure but does not, by itself, establish a unique physical observer, a unique coordinate frame, or a nuclear operator representation.

### A5. Finite Transition Window

The architecture postulates a finite transition interval:

$$
\tau_{Ji} > 0, \quad (10)
$$

during which an effective metric-stiffness or barrier parameter may be varied. The physical meaning of this interval depends on the model's time variable, its dynamical equations, and the operational definition of the stiffness. A5 is a modeling assumption whose measurable or computable content remains to be supplied.

### A6. Non-Hermitian Effective Drive

The model introduces an effective non-Hermitian operator:

$$
H_{\mathrm{eff}}(\tau) = H_{\mathrm{real}} + i\Gamma(\tau)\hat K, \quad (11)
$$

where $H_{\mathrm{real}}$ is Hermitian, $\hat K$ is a specified Hermitian operator on the same Hilbert space, and $\Gamma(\tau)$ has units chosen so that $\Gamma\hat K$ has units of energy.

In the geometric representation, one may seek to construct $\hat K$ from the square of the skew-symmetric generator $A$. Since $A^{T} = -A$, the matrix $A^{2}$ is real symmetric and negative semidefinite. However, $A^{2}$ acts on the original three-dimensional geometric space. It cannot be inserted into a nuclear Hamiltonian until a representation map into the nuclear Hilbert space has been defined (Section 6.3). The sign of the imaginary term also matters: for $\Gamma \geq 0$ and a negative-semidefinite $\hat K$, the convention describes loss rather than gain in the corresponding effective evolution. A physical model must state its sign convention and identify the source of the non-Hermiticity.

### B1. Proposed Cross-Scale Bridge

Let $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{model}}$ and $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{nuclear}}$ denote specified Hilbert spaces, or specified invariant subspaces of larger Hilbert spaces. A candidate linear map is:

$$
\Phi: \mathcal H_{\mathrm{model}} \rightarrow \mathcal H_{\mathrm{nuclear}}. \quad (12)
$$

If $\hat W_{\mathrm{model}}$ and $\hat W_{\mathrm{nuclear}}$ are well-defined operators on the corresponding spaces, a proposed intertwining condition is:

$$
\hat W_{\mathrm{nuclear}}\Phi = \Phi\hat W_{\mathrm{model}}, \quad (13)
$$

on a stated common domain.

This relation is not automatically an isomorphism; it does not require $\Phi^{-1}$ unless $\Phi$ is proved bijective on the relevant subspaces. The map's linearity, domain, range, inner-product properties, and compatibility with the relevant dynamics must be established. B1 is a research postulate, not a demonstrated nuclear correspondence.

$$

and

$$
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{Zhi}}(\theta) = C_{\mathrm{Cong}} \circ R(\theta). \quad (16)
$$

Here $\mathcal M$ is a state or configuration space to be specified, $R(\theta)$ is the rotation of A4, and $C_{\mathrm{Cong}}$ is a boundary-selection map associated with the proposed Silicon Jade Cong architecture.

### 3.2 The Creator as an Oriented Operator Structure

In this paper, "creator" is used in a formal, non-theological sense. It does not denote a person, a consciousness, or a deity. It denotes an oriented operator architecture consisting of an origin anchor, a generator choice, and an ordered construction procedure.

| Aspect | Intentional-subject interpretation | `emind` operator interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | A person or agent | A specified origin in a state space |
| Direction | Psychological intention | A selected generator and orientation |
| Action | A voluntary sequence | A sequence of operator applications |

The `emind` architecture has **orientation** in the mathematical sense—a choice of generator, a direction of transformation, and a target condition—but it does not thereby possess psychological intention. This distinction prevents a geometric construction proposal from being misread as a claim about consciousness or theology. The creator is the oriented operator structure, not an intending subject.

## 4. The Five-Stage Construction Chain for Gold-197

The target benchmark is stable $^{197}\mathrm{Au}$, with $Z = 79$ and $N = 118$. The construction is divided into five stages. At this stage, each stage is a formal specification with a verification obligation; the criteria below are not reported as completed numerical results.

### 4.1 Stage P1: Target Encoding

The target is encoded by a tuple:

$$
\mathcal T_{\mathrm{Au}} = \left( \mathcal R_{\mathrm{Au}}, W_{\mathrm{Au}}, \mathcal Q_{\mathrm{Au}} \right), \quad (18)
$$

where $\mathcal R_{\mathrm{Au}}$ is the curvature variable assigned by the MES model, $W_{\mathrm{Au}}$ is a proposed topological label if a suitable nuclear invariant can be defined, and $\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{Au}}$ denotes the remaining quantum numbers and constraints needed to identify the target state.

From A1, with reference quantities fixed:

$$
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{Au}} = R_0 \left(\frac{m_0}{m_{\mathrm{Au}}}\right)^2. \quad (19)
$$

This is a model-level encoding; it does not independently determine nuclear structure or establish that curvature is the physical origin of the measured mass.

A three-dimensional winding-number expression is:

$$
W[g] = \frac{1}{24\pi^2} \int_{S^3} \operatorname{Tr}\left[(g^{-1}dg)^3\right], \quad (20)
$$

for a sufficiently smooth map $g: S^3 \rightarrow SU(2)$, with sign and normalization fixed by convention. Under appropriate conditions this is an integer-valued homotopy invariant. It is not, by itself, a nuclear topological charge. To use it for Gold-197, the model must define the relevant configuration manifold, the map $g$, and a justified correspondence to a nuclear invariant. No value of $W_{\mathrm{Au}}$ is assigned in this paper.

**Verification obligation P1.** The target encoding must be well-defined on a specified state space and invariant under admissible changes of representation. If uniqueness is claimed, it must be proved as uniqueness of the target equivalence class under stated constraints, not as uniqueness of a written formula.

### 4.2 Stage P2: Path Planning

A path is specified from an admissible initial state to the target. For a curvature variable $R(\tau)$, A1 implies:

$$
\frac{dm}{d\tau} = -\frac{1}{2}m_0 R_0^{1/2} R^{-3/2} \frac{dR}{d\tau}. \quad (21)
$$

This is the derivative of the proposed curvature–mass relation, not an independent dynamical equation. A physical trajectory requires an equation for $R(\tau)$, its initial and boundary conditions, and a mechanism that produces the trajectory.

The nuclear benchmark requires explicit attention to particle-number sectors. The conventional sequence:

$$
{}^{198}_{80}\mathrm{Hg}(n,2n){}^{197}_{80}\mathrm{Hg}, \quad (22)
$$

followed by electron capture:

$$
{}^{197}_{80}\mathrm{Hg} + e^- \rightarrow {}^{197}_{79}\mathrm{Au} + \nu_e, \quad (23)
$$

changes both the nuclear species and the particle content of the relevant sectors. It cannot be represented as a path within a single fixed-$(A,Z)$ nuclear Hilbert space. A formal construction that includes such processes must use a larger state space—such as a direct sum over the relevant particle-number and charge sectors—and define the reaction operators between them.

If a topological label is eventually defined, let:

$$
\Delta W = W_{\mathrm{Au}} - W_{\mathrm{initial}}. \quad (24)
$$

A nonzero $\Delta W$ indicates that the endpoints belong to different classes under the chosen invariant. Whether a continuous path is forbidden, whether the state space must be enlarged, or whether an open-system transition can connect the sectors must be established from the topology and dynamics of the actual model. A discrete collapse is not assumed merely because $\Delta W \neq 0$.

**Verification obligation P2.** Specify the state space, the admissible path, the dynamical generator, initial and boundary conditions, and endpoint constraints. Verify that the path exists in the stated model and reaches the target sector. The curvature derivative alone does not satisfy this obligation.

### 4.3 Stage P3: Window Transition

A5 introduces a finite transition interval $\tau_{Ji}$. Within this interval, the effective Hamiltonian may be varied according to a specified protocol. The non-Hermitian form is the effective Hamiltonian of Eq. (11) in A6:

$$
H_{\mathrm{eff}}(\tau) = H_{\mathrm{real}} + i\Gamma(\tau)\hat K. \quad (25)
$$

An exceptional point (EP) is a parameter value at which eigenvalues and eigenvectors coalesce so that the operator becomes defective. Its existence depends on the detailed operator family. In a generic complex matrix family, an EP typically has real codimension 2: two real parameters must be tuned, unless a symmetry or other constraint reduces the codimension. A one-parameter sweep must therefore not be assumed to encounter an EP. If a symmetry reduces the codimension to 1, the symmetry must be stated and its protection verified.

The winding of a parameter-space loop around an EP and a nuclear topological charge are distinct quantities. Let $\nu_{\mathrm{EP}}$ denote a suitably defined parameter-space winding number. A possible bridge would have the form:

$$
\Delta W_{\mathrm{nuclear}} = \mathcal F(\nu_{\mathrm{EP}}, \Phi), \quad (26)
$$

where $\mathcal F$ must be constructed and tested. The identity $\Delta W_{\mathrm{nuclear}} = \nu_{\mathrm{EP}}$ is not assumed.

**Verification obligation P3.** Establish the existence and location of the EP for a specified operator family; demonstrate that the parameter loop is well-defined; test numerical stability and eigenvector coalescence; and independently derive any claimed relation to a nuclear invariant. Sampling density and condition-number thresholds must be specified before numerical results are interpreted.

**Methodological analogy: the "Wheelwright Pian" dynamic window.** The Zhuangzi's parable of Wheelwright Pian (*Tiandao*) illustrates that mastery over a physical transformation cannot be reduced to static written prescriptions ("the dregs of the ancients"), but resides in a precise, countable rhythm (*shu*, 数) within the dynamic interval—neither too fast (*bu xu*, 不徐) nor too slow (*bu ji*, 不疾). Within the present constructive architecture, this classical insight serves strictly as a heuristic analogy for the non-emptiness condition of the finite transition window $\tau_{Ji} > 0$ (A5 and Appendix A.8). Specifically, $\tau_{Ji}$ is bounded from below by the adiabatic or Landau–Zener timescale $\tau_{\mathrm{LZ}}$ (preventing non-adiabatic leakage out of the target manifold if driven too rapidly) and from above by the bound-state environmental decoherence timescale $T_2 \approx 1/\gamma_{\mathrm{env}}^{\mathrm{bound}}$ (preventing phase collapse if evolved too slowly):

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{LZ}} < \tau_{Ji} < \frac{1}{\gamma_{\mathrm{env}}^{\mathrm{bound}}(T)}. \quad (27)
$$

Here $\gamma_{\mathrm{env}}^{\mathrm{bound}}$ is the environmental decoherence rate (inverse time), consistent with the convention of Section 6.5. Consistent with Principle P1, this analogy does not substitute for physical derivation; rather, it clarifies why static open-loop formulas fail and why the existence of a nonempty operating interval depends critically on quantifying $\gamma_{\mathrm{env}}^{\mathrm{bound}}(T)$ and the minimum spectral gap.

### 4.4 Stage P4: Condensation

A2 proposes that nonlinear feedback may stabilize a selected target state. A schematic evolution equation is:

$$
i\hbar \frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\tau} = \left[ H_{\mathrm{eff}}(\tau) + \mathcal N_{\mathrm{sat}}[\Psi] \right]\Psi, \quad (28)
$$

where $\mathcal N_{\mathrm{sat}}$ is a nonlinear term intended to represent saturation and feedback.

The nonlinear term must be explicitly defined before the equation can be analyzed. A real cubic term proportional to $|\Psi|^2$ produces a nonlinear phase or energy shift in a standard Schrödinger equation; it does not, by itself, establish dissipative saturation or a stable attractor. A dissipative saturation mechanism requires a specified imaginary contribution or an equivalent open-system formulation, with its sign and physical origin stated.

A critical parameter $\Gamma_c$ may be defined only after stationary solutions and their stability have been analyzed. Its existence, uniqueness, and bifurcation type are not assumed.

**Verification obligation P4.** Specify $\mathcal N_{\mathrm{sat}}$, its parameters and dimensions, the state normalization, and boundary conditions. Find stationary or periodic solutions, linearize the dynamics, and establish stability properties. A stable global attractor requires a separate argument and does not follow solely from adding a nonlinear term.

### 4.5 Stage P5: Stabilization

The final stage reshapes the effective landscape and keeps the evolving state within a target neighborhood. If $\hat W$ is a well-defined Hermitian operator and $\rho$ is a physical density operator, a candidate target function is:

$$
h(\rho) = \Omega_{\mathrm{target}} - \operatorname{Tr}\left[ \rho(\hat W - W_{\mathrm{Au}}I)^2 \right]. \quad (29)
$$

Here $W_{\mathrm{Au}}$ is a specified target value, $I$ is the identity, and $\Omega_{\mathrm{target}} \geq 0$ has the same dimensions as the trace expectation. The admissible target neighborhood is:

$$
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{target}} = \{\rho : h(\rho) \geq 0\}. \quad (30)
$$

A control-barrier condition is:

$$
\dot h(\rho(\tau)) + \alpha(h(\rho(\tau))) \geq 0, \quad (31)
$$

where $\alpha$ belongs to an extended class-$\mathcal K$ function. This condition can support forward invariance only when the state dynamics, control inputs, regularity assumptions, and feasibility conditions have been specified.

For a quantum system, the density-operator dynamics and the admissible control channels must be defined explicitly. A classical control-barrier inequality cannot simply be asserted to prove quantum-state stabilization.

**Verification obligation P5.** Define the density-operator evolution, the control channel, the target observable, and the admissible control set. Prove or numerically test forward invariance of the target neighborhood under stated assumptions. If fidelity is used, define it and report the initial state, target state, and evolution protocol. No fidelity value is claimed in this paper.

## 5. The Verification System

### 5.1 Verification Obligations and Numerical Diagnostics

The five stages can be investigated using simplified finite-dimensional models before any nuclear-scale interpretation is attempted. The following diagnostics are proposed as numerical checks. They are acceptance criteria to be specified and justified for each implementation, not results already obtained.

| Diagnostic | Quantity to evaluate | Required definition or condition |
|---|---|---|
| Projection idempotence | $\|\Pi_0^2 - \Pi_0\|_F$ | Apply only if $\Pi_0$ is explicitly defined as a projection |
| Biorthogonal normalization | $\lvert\langle L_n\vert R_m\rangle - \delta_{nm}\rvert$ | Specify left and right eigenvector normalization |
| Physical density-operator validity | Hermiticity, positivity, trace | Check for the physical density operator $\rho$ |
| EP resolution | Eigenvalue splitting and eigenvector coalescence | Define parameter grid, tolerance, condition-number test |
| Target conversion | $F(\rho, \rho_{\mathrm{target}})$ | Define fidelity convention and evolution protocol |
| Trace preservation | $\lvert\operatorname{Tr}\rho(\tau) - 1\rvert$ | Apply to trace-preserving physical dynamics |

The projection test applies only to an operator explicitly defined as a projection. It does not imply that the `emind` map or $C_{\mathrm{Cong}}$ is idempotent. These are distinct mathematical objects unless a construction identifies them.

For a non-Hermitian operator, left and right eigenvectors may be defined by:

$$
H|R_n\rangle = E_n|R_n\rangle, \qquad \langle L_n|H = E_n\langle L_n|. \quad (32)
$$

A biorthogonal normalization convention may be imposed as:

$$
\langle L_n|R_m\rangle = \delta_{nm}, \quad (33)
$$

when the eigenvectors form a suitable complete basis. Near an EP, eigenvector conditioning and completeness require special care.

A biorthogonal representation is not automatically a physical density operator. The physical density operator must satisfy:

$$
\rho^\dagger = \rho, \qquad \rho \geq 0, \qquad \operatorname{Tr}\rho = 1. \quad (34)
$$

A biorthogonal auxiliary object need not satisfy all of these properties. The two representations must not be conflated.

For a Lindblad evolution, the standard dissipator associated with a jump operator $\Pi_0$ is:

$$
\mathcal D[\rho] = \kappa \left( \Pi_0\rho\Pi_0^\dagger - \frac{1}{2} \{\Pi_0^\dagger\Pi_0, \rho\} \right), \quad (35)
$$

where $\kappa \geq 0$ and $\{\cdot,\cdot\}$ is the anticommutator. Both anticommutator contributions are required for the standard trace-preserving Lindblad form; omitting the $\rho\Pi_0^\dagger\Pi_0$ term breaks trace conservation and is not a valid dissipator. The full generator must also be specified before trace preservation or complete positivity can be claimed.

### 5.2 Failure Modes

The Creating Gold paradigm is verifiable at the level of its explicitly defined models. The following failure modes identify ways in which a proposed implementation may fail.

**F1. Incompatible topological invariants.** If a model-space invariant and a proposed nuclear invariant cannot be related by a well-defined map on the stated domains, B1 fails for that implementation. A compensating term must not be introduced merely to force agreement; any extension must be independently defined and tested.

**F2. No exceptional point in the specified parameter domain.** If a complete and numerically controlled search finds no defective degeneracy in the stated operator family and parameter domain, the proposed EP mechanism is unsupported for that model. A near-degeneracy or quasi-EP may be studied as a different mechanism, but it must not be described as an EP. Because a generic EP has codimension 2, a one-parameter scan is insufficient unless a symmetry is stated and verified.

**F3. No controlled cross-scale relation.** If no stable and independently constrained mapping connects the model-space drive to a nuclear effective operator, the nuclear interpretation remains undefined. Replacing a single scale function with an unconstrained family does not solve the problem; additional data or theoretical constraints are required.

**F4. Failure of nonlinear stabilization.** If the proposed nonlinear dynamics has no stable target solution under the stated conditions, P4 fails for that implementation.

**F5. Failure of target-set invariance.** If the control law cannot keep trajectories within the specified target set, P5 fails for that implementation.

A failed test on one specified target or model invalidates the particular implementation and any general claim that logically depends on it. It does not automatically refute every possible state-space construction.

### 5.3 The Global Topological Curvature Network as a Verification Geometry

The GTCN is the global geometric representation of the construction and verification network. It is intended to encode the five construction stages, the five physical research channels, their interconnections, and the unresolved parts of the network in one geometric object. Its status in the MES architecture is not downgraded by the fact that its complete mathematical realization remains a construction task.

#### 5.3.1 Geometric data and fixed four-dimensional structure

Let $\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}$ be a smooth, oriented, four-dimensional manifold ($d_{\mathrm{GTC}} = 4$), possibly with three-dimensional boundary $\partial\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}$, equipped with a metric $g$, a compatible connection $\nabla$, and the corresponding Riemann curvature tensor $R^{(\mathcal N)}_{abcd}$. The dimension $d_{\mathrm{GTC}} = 4$ is fixed identically across the sister-paper pair. The ambient MES manifold $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{web}}$ is the mother layer of Section 1.2; its local ambient sector is taken to have dimension $D_{\mathrm{MES}} = 5$. The codimension-one embedding

$$
\iota:\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathrm{web}} \quad (36)
$$

specifies the unit normal field $n$, induced metric $g_{ab}$, and second fundamental form $K_{ab}$. (If a higher-dimensional ambient sector $D_{\mathrm{MES}} > 5$ is used, the normal bundle, normal connection, and Ricci equations must be supplied accordingly.) The channel data are mapped into this four-dimensional geometric structure by explicitly defined maps $\Psi_i$; a channel is not a curvature source merely by being assigned a label.

#### 5.3.2 Gauss–Codazzi–Ricci compatibility

The compatibility equations for the codimension-one embedding were first stated as Eqs. (1)–(2) in Section 1.2. With the four-dimensional GTCN of Section 5.3.1 embedded in the five-dimensional ambient sector of $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{web}}$, they take the explicit form

$$
R^{(M)}_{abcd}\big|_{\mathcal N} = R^{(\mathcal N)}_{abcd} + \varepsilon(K_{ac}K_{bd} - K_{ad}K_{bc}), \quad (37)
$$

$$
\nabla_a K_{bc} - \nabla_b K_{ac} = R^{(M)}_{n cab}, \quad (38)
$$

with $\varepsilon=\pm1$ determined by the normal signature and the adopted curvature convention. In higher codimension, the normal index, normal connection, and Ricci equation are required. The scalar Gauss relation is a contraction of the tensor equation under specified assumptions; it is not the complete embedding condition. The compatibility equations are necessary geometric constraints, not a proof by themselves that every MES postulate or physical channel is realized.

#### 5.3.3 Four-dimensional curvature functional and topological invariant

The original schematic expression $\int_{\mathcal N}\mathcal R\,dV + \int_{\partial\mathcal N}\mathcal K\,dA$ is not a topological invariant in four dimensions. With $d_{\mathrm{GTC}} = 4$ ($m = 2$), the GTCN uses the canonical four-dimensional Gauss–Bonnet–Chern characteristic formula for a compact oriented Riemannian 4-manifold:

$$
\chi(\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}) = \int_{\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}}\mathcal E_{4}(g) + \int_{\partial\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}}\mathcal B_{3}(g,K), \quad (39)
$$

where $\mathcal E_4(g)$ is the normalized four-dimensional Euler curvature form (quadratic in the Riemann curvature tensor, proportional to $\varepsilon^{abcd}\varepsilon^{efgh}R_{abef}R_{cdgh}\,dV_4 / (32\pi^2)$ in Riemannian signature, or its declared pseudo-Riemannian counterpart) and $\mathcal B_3(g,K)$ is the corresponding three-dimensional boundary transgression form constructed from the induced metric and the second fundamental form $K_{ab}$. (For comparison, in a two-dimensional reduction $m=1$, this reduces to the standard Gauss–Bonnet formula $\chi = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int R\,dA + \frac{1}{2\pi}\int k_g\,ds$.) Fixing $d_{\mathrm{GTC}} = 4$ ensures that the bulk invariant depends quadratically on curvature, capturing multi-channel curvature couplings that linear scalar integrals cannot resolve.

#### 5.3.4 Known, unresolved, and interface regions

An unresolved channel is not automatically a geometric boundary. Let $\mathcal N=\mathcal N_{K}\cup\mathcal N_{U}$, where $\mathcal N_K$ is the region supported by evaluated data and $\mathcal N_U$ is the unresolved region. If their interface is a regular hypersurface $\Sigma=\partial\mathcal N_K\cap\partial\mathcal N_U$, its induced geometry and matching conditions may enter a boundary or interface functional. The boundary term is included only when the geometric decomposition and boundary conditions justify it. Unresolved information must otherwise remain an explicit unknown field or parameter, not be silently converted into boundary curvature.

#### 5.3.5 Relation to the five-channel evidence state

The GTCN can encode the joint state of the channels, but a topological invariant is not assumed to correspond one-to-one to a finite list of evidence verdicts. The evidence state is represented directly by a vector

$$
\mathbf s=(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4,s_5), \qquad s_i\in\{A,R,U\}, \quad (40)
$$

where $A$ means supported within the declared model domain, $R$ means ruled out within that domain, and $U$ means unresolved. The GTCN can carry this vector as channel-labelled data and can investigate whether distinct vectors belong to distinct geometric or topological classes. Such a correspondence is a theorem to be established, not a premise.

#### 5.3.6 What the GTCN adds

The GTCN supplies the global geometric layer in which local diagnostics, channel couplings, and unresolved regions can be represented together. Its contribution is stronger than a list of channels: it makes their geometric relationships, embedding constraints, and possible global invariants explicit. The precise characteristic functional and channel maps are part of the mathematical construction and are to be verified in the fixed dimension shared by the sister papers.

## 6. The Cross-Scale Bridge B1 and Its Nuclear Requirements

### 6.1 Formal Structure

B1 is an intertwining relation between a model-space structure and a nuclear-space structure:

$$
\hat W_{\mathrm{nuclear}}\Phi = \Phi\hat W_{\mathrm{model}}, \quad (41)
$$

on a specified domain, provided both operators and $\Phi$ are rigorously defined.

A second, dynamical correspondence may be sought:

$$
\Phi\left(i\Gamma\hat K_{\mathrm{model}}\right) \approx -\frac{i}{2}\mathcal W_{\mathrm{eff}}, \quad (42)
$$

where $\mathcal W_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is a nuclear effective operator with energy dimensions. This is a proposed effective correspondence, not an exact isomorphism. It requires a stated projection, truncation, or renormalization procedure and a controlled error estimate. In particular, a geometric operator acting in a three-dimensional representation cannot be identified with a nuclear quadrupole interaction or a continuum-coupling operator merely by matching notation. The two operators may act on different spaces, have different particle ranks, and encode different physical mechanisms.

### 6.2 Gold-197 and the $^{208}\mathrm{Pb}$ Reference Core

Gold-197 has $Z = 79$ and $N = 118$. Relative to doubly magic $^{208}\mathrm{Pb}$, it is described at the bookkeeping level as three proton holes and eight neutron holes. This does not by itself determine the wave function or the dominant configuration.

For proton holes below $Z = 82$, a conventional shell-model space includes the orbitals:

$$
\mathcal H_\pi = \operatorname{span}\{d_{5/2}, g_{7/2}, s_{1/2}, d_{3/2}, h_{11/2}\}, \quad (43)
$$

with radial-node labels and ordering specified by the chosen single-particle convention. A corresponding neutron-hole space below $N = 126$ may be built from:

$$
\mathcal H_\nu = \operatorname{span}\{h_{9/2}, f_{7/2}, f_{5/2}, p_{3/2}, p_{1/2}, i_{13/2}\}. \quad (44)
$$

The precise truncation and effective interaction must be specified and justified for the calculation.

The electric quadrupole operator has tensor rank 2 and even parity. Its single-particle selection rules include:

$$
|j_a - j_b| \leq 2 \leq j_a + j_b, \qquad \pi_a\pi_b = +1, \quad (45)
$$

together with the orbital angular-momentum triangle condition. These rules determine whether a matrix element is allowed by angular momentum and parity; they do not determine its magnitude or establish dynamical dominance.

For example, the $h_{11/2} \rightarrow d_{3/2}$ transition does not satisfy the rank-2 orbital condition because $|5 - 2| = 3$. The $h_{11/2} \rightarrow g_{7/2}$ transition fails the E2 parity condition because the two orbitals have opposite parity. The $h_{11/2} \rightarrow f_{7/2}$ transition satisfies the single-particle E2 angular-momentum and parity rules ($|5 - 3| = 2$, $|11/2 - 7/2| = 2$, same parity), but $f_{7/2}$ belongs to a different shell sector from the proton-hole space below $Z = 82$. It therefore requires an explicitly enlarged model space or a cross-shell treatment and must not be presented as an ordinary transition wholly within the proton-hole space. Within the proton-hole shell, candidate E2 transitions among positive-parity orbitals such as $d_{5/2} \leftrightarrow g_{7/2}$ may be considered, subject to the detailed many-body configuration and radial calculation.

### 6.3 Woods-Saxon Radial Matrix Elements

A Woods-Saxon calculation may be used to estimate radial wave functions and matrix elements, provided the potential is fully specified. At minimum, the calculation must report the central potential depth and proton/neutron convention, radius parameter and mass-number dependence, surface diffuseness, spin-orbit strength and radial form, Coulomb potential for protons, radial boundary conditions and numerical grid, single-particle energy convention, radial normalization, and matrix-element definition.

A quadrupole radial factor may be written schematically as:

$$
\mathcal M_{ab}^{\mathrm{rad}} = \int_0^\infty u_a(r)\,r^2\,u_b(r)\,dr, \quad (46)
$$

where $u_a$ and $u_b$ are normalized according to the solver's convention. The full reduced matrix element also contains angular-momentum coefficients and effective-charge or operator-renormalization factors.

No numerical Woods-Saxon radial matrix element is asserted in this paper. A solver must be benchmarked against evaluated single-particle or spectroscopic information appropriate to the nucleus and model space before its outputs are used in the cross-scale argument. For $^{197}\mathrm{Au}$, the $1h_{11/2}$ proton-hole state is the 409-keV isomer with $J^\pi = 11/2^-$ and $T_{1/2} = 7.73\ \mathrm{s}$; its binding energy relative to the proton separation energy $S_p$ constrains the potential. The ground state is $3/2^+$. These data provide the benchmark against which any solver must be checked.

### 6.4 Effective Quadrupole Coupling

A quadrupole–quadrupole interaction may be represented schematically as:

$$
H_{QQ} = -\chi\,\hat Q\cdot\hat Q, \quad (47)
$$

where the dimensions of $\chi$ depend on the normalization of $\hat Q$. If $\hat Q$ has dimensions of length squared, $\chi$ has dimensions of energy per length to the fourth power. The numerical value of $\chi$ is model-dependent and cannot be inferred from a dimensional estimate alone; it must be calibrated or derived using a specified nuclear interaction, model space, effective charges, radial wave functions, and data set. Accordingly, this paper does not adopt a numerical value for $G_{\mathrm{eff}}$, $\chi$, a nuclear quadrupole-operator norm, or a non-Hermitian nuclear coupling strength.

### 6.5 Nuclear Continuum Coupling and Environmental Decoherence

The Gamow Shell Model uses a Berggren ensemble that can include bound states, resonant states, and nonresonant continuum contributions. Its non-Hermitian effective description is relevant when coupling to open decay channels is physically important. It does not follow that every bound valence state has an intrinsic non-Hermitian width of the same kind.

The $^{197}\mathrm{Au}$ ground state is stable, and the 409-keV isomer is an evaluated level. A bound-state calculation must distinguish four quantities that are not interchangeable:

1. the nuclear separation energy and the location of a state relative to particle-emission thresholds;
2. a resonance or continuum-coupling width in an open-channel description;
3. environmental decoherence caused by interactions with electrons, radiation, lattice degrees of freedom, or an external bath;
4. phenomenological non-Hermitian terms introduced as part of a control model.

In particular, a continuum-coupling width calculated for a resonance is not automatically the environmental decoherence rate of a bound nuclear configuration. If $\gamma_{\mathrm{env}}$ denotes a decoherence rate, its units are inverse time; the associated energy scale is $\Delta_{\mathrm{env}} = \hbar\gamma_{\mathrm{env}}$. A dimensionally consistent drive-gain estimate would compare energy scales:

$$
\Gamma_{\mathrm{drive}} \gtrsim \frac{\hbar\gamma_{\mathrm{env}}}{\left|\langle\hat H_{\mathrm{drive}}\rangle_{\mathrm{scale}}\right|}, \quad (48)
$$

where the denominator is a properly defined drive-induced energy scale. This expression is schematic; the environmental coupling model and the drive operator must be supplied before a numerical gain can be estimated. The environmental decoherence rate for the relevant nuclear–atomic–environment system has not been calculated here, and no numerical range or drive-gain order of magnitude is claimed.

For a system such as Gold-197, whose valence orbits are all bound, the intrinsic continuum coupling is not activated in the low-lying valence space. A logically necessary candidate channel is **external driving**: if a strong field resonant with the nuclear quadrupole degree of freedom exists, the induced non-Hermitian coupling may be raised to the order of $\gamma_{\mathrm{env}}^{\mathrm{bound}}$. This channel has not been confirmed by any calculation or experiment. It is a **verifiable engineering channel**: whether it holds depends on a computable quantity—given the external field parameters, how many times $\gamma_{\mathrm{env}}^{\mathrm{bound}}$ can the induced non-Hermitian coupling reach. If this ratio is far below 1, then for Gold-197 Type 3 can be judged negative; if it approaches or exceeds 1, then Type 3 moves from suspension to an experimentally testable candidate.

### 6.6 Exceptional-Point Resolution and Temperature

If an EP-based protocol is proposed, the relevant spectral gap, parameter-space distance from the EP, and numerical conditioning must be defined for the chosen effective Hamiltonian. A generic EP has codimension 2; unless a symmetry reduces it, the parameter space must be at least two-dimensional. A one-parameter temperature or drive sweep cannot be assumed to cross an EP.

A generic energy-window estimate cannot be converted into a temperature limit without a model of thermal populations, line broadening, noise spectra, and measurement or control resolution. For reference:

$$
k_BT = 8.617333262 \times 10^{-5}\,T\ \mathrm{eV}, \quad (49)
$$

when $T$ is in kelvin. This conversion alone does not determine whether a particular EP feature is thermally resolvable. No EP exclusion-zone width or cryogenic operating temperature is claimed as established in this paper.

### 6.7 Five Channels, Physical Coupling, and the Evidence-State Vector

The five channels are retained as the L2 research network within the GTCN. They are heterogeneous in physical role, but they are not discarded or reduced: together they cover candidate mechanisms, reaction-sector connectivity, and environmental characterization required by the target construction.

| Channel | Research role | Principal mathematical object | Main verification question |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 Deformation | Candidate activation mechanism | Deformation-dependent nuclear Hamiltonian and resonance poles | Can a relevant state approach or cross an emission threshold within a defined parameter domain? |
| C2 Rotation | Candidate activation mechanism | Cranked Hamiltonian and Coriolis matrix elements | Does rotation create quantitatively significant state mixing and continuum coupling? |
| C3 Strong-field dressing | Candidate activation mechanism | Nuclear multipole drive and Floquet operator | Can driven states couple to open particle-emission channels with useful partial widths? |
| C4 Particle-number sectors | Reaction-network and state-space construction | Direct-sum channel Hilbert space and inter-sector reaction operators | Does a physically allowed sequence of reactions connect the initial sector to the target sector? |
| C5 Environment and internal conversion | Environmental and decay-channel characterization | Open-system master equation and decay jump operators | How do environmental coupling, internal conversion, radiation, and other processes affect coherence, lifetime, and branching? |

#### 6.7.1 Direct-sum reaction-channel space

Let $\alpha$ label a complete reaction channel, including the nuclear species $(A,Z)$, internal nuclear state, relative-motion quantum numbers, and any emitted or absorbed particles. Define

$$
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{ch}} = \bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal C}\mathcal H_\alpha, \qquad |\Psi\rangle=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal C}|\psi_\alpha\rangle. \quad (50)
$$

The channel Hamiltonian has a block structure

$$
H_{\mathrm{ch}}= \begin{pmatrix} H_{11}&V_{12}&\cdots\\ V_{21}&H_{22}&\cdots\\ \vdots&\vdots&\ddots \end{pmatrix}, \qquad V_{\alpha\beta}=P_\alpha H P_\beta. \quad (51)
$$

The direct sum places sectors in a common mathematical state space. Physical transitions require nonzero off-diagonal couplings $V_{\alpha\beta}$ generated by a specified interaction or reaction operator. The direct sum alone does not create a reaction path.

A candidate path $\alpha_0\to\alpha_1\to\cdots\to\alpha_f$ is admissible only if every edge satisfies the relevant conservation laws, has a nonzero coupling matrix element, is energetically open or has a properly defined virtual contribution, and yields a nonzero transition amplitude under the coupled dynamics. For nuclear reactions, charge and baryon number conservation apply to the complete system, including emitted particles; energy, angular momentum, parity, and other quantum numbers must be checked channel by channel.

#### 6.7.2 From channel space to a reaction path

The path search is formulated on a directed weighted graph $\mathcal G_{\mathrm{rxn}}=(\mathcal V,\mathcal E)$. Vertices represent nuclear states and open reaction channels. Edges represent physically specified interactions, decays, captures, transfers, or particle-emission processes. Edge weights are computed from transition amplitudes, partial widths, cross sections, or transition probabilities. A graph edge is not admitted solely because two nuclei are adjacent in the nuclear chart.

For a proposed path, the coupled-channel equations or an equivalent scattering/transport formulation determine its dynamical reachability. The resulting path must be checked against competing channels and the total probability or flux balance. AI-assisted graph search can discover candidate paths, but a candidate is physically established only after the corresponding operators, matrix elements, thresholds, and branching probabilities have been evaluated.

#### 6.7.3 Five-channel evidence-state vector

The former four-state classification is replaced by the five-channel evidence-state vector

$$
\mathbf s=(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4,s_5), \qquad s_i\in\{A,R,U\}. \quad (52)
$$

- $A$: supported within the explicitly stated model and parameter domain;
- $R$: ruled out within that same declared domain;
- $U$: unresolved, uncomputed, or insufficiently constrained.

The vector is a product evidence space, not a four-state global feasibility label. Its $3^5$ formal combinations record the independent status of five channels. They do not automatically decide Type 3. In particular, support for one candidate mechanism is not equivalent to a complete end-to-end physical reaction pathway. A Type-3 positive conclusion requires a connected, conservation-respecting, quantitatively viable path from the initial state to the target, including target-state population and independent identification. A negative conclusion may be made only for the specified mechanisms and model domains; the five channels are not asserted to exhaust every conceivable physical process.

#### 6.7.4 The Bottleneck Loop as a testable physical-cycle hypothesis

The Bottleneck Loop is retained as a central physical insight: the limiting behavior of one channel may be altered by the state of another, and a closed sequence of such dependencies may produce a collective effect that is absent in isolated-channel calculations.

The proposed directed couplings are:

- C2 $\rightarrow$ C3: rotational mixing may modify the transition matrix elements entering the driven Floquet coupling;
- C4 $\rightarrow$ C1: a reaction path through another nuclear sector may expose a different deformation landscape or intermediate state;
- C5 $\rightarrow$ C2: environmental coupling may alter rotational-state coherence, lifetime, or population transfer; whether this assists or suppresses the process must be calculated;
- C3 $\rightarrow$ C5: driven-state population may change the available internal-conversion and radiative branches, thereby feeding back into the environmental dynamics;
- C1 $\rightarrow$ C4: deformation-dependent level structure may alter the accessibility and branching of a neighbouring-sector reaction.

Each edge is a physical hypothesis requiring a defined coupling term, parameter domain, predicted sign and magnitude, and a falsification condition. One candidate closed feedback cycle is C2 $\rightarrow$ C3 $\rightarrow$ C5 $\rightarrow$ C2; a second candidate cycle is C1 $\leftrightarrow$ C4. The loop is not assumed to enhance every process; feedback can be constructive or destructive. Its novelty lies in treating the channels as a coupled dynamical network rather than independent tests.

A normalized combination metric may be defined only after the same initial and final states, normalization, parameter domain, and observable are used for all protocols. If $\Gamma$ denotes an energy width, all terms in a ratio must have energy units; if it denotes a rate, all terms must have inverse-time units. A value above unity indicates enhancement of the chosen metric, not by itself an increase in experimentally measured target yield. The concrete form of this metric, together with its comparison protocol, is given in the sister paper DW58553929, Section 6.3.

#### 6.7.5 Channel roles and verification obligations

The five channels involve different mathematical tasks: C1 includes threshold and resonance-pole searches; C2 requires rotating-frame mixing and continuum matrix elements; C3 requires Floquet and open-channel calculations; C4 requires reaction-network construction and inter-sector transition operators; C5 requires environmental and decay-channel calculations. These tasks are complementary and can be run in parallel. Their results populate the evidence-state vector and the GTCN representation.

## 8. Ricci Flow and Variational Control

The static curvature functional of Section 5.3.3 is a starting point, not the full theory. Two dynamic extensions are available, both currently open.

### 8.1 Ricci Flow

A dynamic extension is the Ricci flow:

$$
\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial t} = -2R_{\mu\nu}, \quad (53)
$$

under which the metric evolves according to its Ricci tensor. The effect on passage and bottleneck regions depends on the full curvature tensor, boundary conditions, and flow normalization; the signs of scalar curvature alone do not determine a universal opening or closing behavior. The stationary points of this flow are candidate "balanced" configurations in which passage and bottleneck coexist without global disconnection. Whether the Ricci flow of the GTCN converges to such a stationary point, and whether that point corresponds to a "gold-reachable" topological class, is an open mathematical problem.

### 8.2 Variational Control

Given a target topological class for the GTCN characteristic functional of Eq. (39) in Section 5.3.3, the variational problem:

$$
\frac{\delta\mathcal{T}[\mathcal{N}_{\text{GTC}}]}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}} = 0 \quad (54)
$$

defines a minimum topological retrofitting of the network: the smallest change to the channel configuration that moves the network into the target class. This is the natural formalization of "which bottleneck should be opened first." Solving it requires the full dynamic theory of Section 8.1, and is therefore an open problem. Only the static functional and the Gauss–Codazzi–Ricci compatibility system are used in this paper.

## 9. The Boundary of the Claims

This section applies the three Gold Truths of Section 1.2, the two guiding principles of Section 1.4, and the Rule of Level-Appropriate Claim to the paper's assertions.

### 9.1 Type 1: Formal Constructive Specification

Type 1 refers to a preliminary formal research architecture in which the proposed construction stages, their intended mathematical roles, and their verification obligations are identified. It does not imply that every operator, representation map, geometric functional, or target-specific construction has already been fully defined or proved.

The present paper specifies the construction chain and the mathematical tasks required to make it operational. The GTCN is retained as a central geometric structure; its complete metric, embedding data, characteristic functional, and channel representation remain explicit construction and verification tasks.

**Level-appropriate claim.** The Type-1 claim concerns the architecture and its stated verification program, not a completed proof of every component. This claim is not withdrawn by the openness of Type 2 or Type 3.

### 9.2 Type 2: Mathematical Model Existence

Type 2 refers to the existence of a mathematical model satisfying the stated axioms and construction requirements. This claim is conditional on the consistency of the axiom set, on the existence of a model with the required properties, and on the satisfaction of the Gauss–Codazzi–Ricci embedding-compatibility system for the GTCN. The absence of a discovered contradiction is not a proof of consistency. No consistency proof is supplied here, and no complete model satisfying A1–A6 and B1 is constructed. Type 2 remains an open mathematical question.

**Level-appropriate claim.** The Type-2 claim is conditional: if the axiom set is consistent, a model is constructed, and the GTCN satisfies the Gauss–Codazzi–Ricci compatibility conditions, then the chain realizes the target. None of these conditions is established in this paper.

### 9.3 Type 3: Physical Realization

Type 3 refers to a correspondence between the proposed construction and a realizable physical process. It requires physically defined degrees of freedom, interactions, initial and boundary conditions, experimentally meaningful observables, and quantitative agreement with established nuclear data.

Type 3 is neither asserted nor denied in this paper. The nuclear realization remains undecided because the required bridge map, nuclear operators, environmental parameters, and control protocol have not been established. Suspension is not denial, and it is not confirmation. It marks a question whose answer remains open pending the required calculations and experiments.

**Level-appropriate claim.** The Type-3 status is unresolved and is represented by the five-channel evidence-state vector (Section 6.7). A Type-3 conclusion requires an end-to-end reaction path and independent physical identification of the target; the evidence state of an individual channel is not sufficient.

### 9.4 How the Two Principles Are Applied

**P1 (against overclaiming) is respected** by the paper's refusal to present any Type-1 or Type-2 result as a Type-3 achievement. The abstract states that no transmutation protocol is claimed as completed. Section 6 states that no Woods-Saxon matrix element, EP realization, decoherence rate, or channel activation is asserted. Section 9.3 states that Type 3 is suspended, not established. These are not hedges; they are the operational content of P1.

This distinction also aligns with a classic methodological caution from the Zhuangzi (*Tiandao*): written words and static notations, divorced from living dynamical coupling, are merely "the dregs of the ancients." Treating a direct-sum Hilbert space label or a formal operator chain (Type 1) as an accomplished nuclear transmutation (Type 3) commits precisely this error. By keeping Type 1 formal specification distinct from Type 3 physical realization, the Dual Principle ensures that mathematical symbols guide—rather than replace—rigorous dynamical verification.

**P2 (against overrejection) is respected** by the paper's refusal to treat the openness of Type 3 as a refutation of the Type-1 architecture. The construction chain is formally specified independently of whether gold is ever produced. The five verification obligations, the GTCN, and the evidence-state structure are statable and testable independently of the physical outcome. Section 9.1 states that the Type-1 claim is not withdrawn by the openness of Type 3. This is the operational content of P2.

**The symmetry.** P1 and P2 are not in tension. Both forbid the same error: treating Type 1 and Type 3 as if they were the same claim. Overclaiming treats a formal construction as a physical achievement; overrejection treats the absence of physical achievement as a failure of the formal construction. The correct response is the same in both cases: keep the levels distinct, state the claim appropriate to each level, and test each level on its own criteria.

In compact form, the two principles can be stated as a single formulation for citation outside this paper:

> **The Dual Principle of Scientific Construction.** Do not claim a physical target that has not yet been realized as a completed scientific discovery, and do not equate a theoretical architecture whose physical realization has not been demonstrated with a lack of mathematical value. Mathematical validity and physical reality must be argued and tested separately.

This compact formulation is a summary of P1 and P2, not an additional principle. It is placed here, after the operational exposition, so that it reads as the distilled statement of what Section 9.4 has already established.

### 9.5 The GTCN and the Treatment of the Unmeasured

The GTCN remains the global geometric child of the MES curvature web. Unresolved channel data are represented explicitly as unknown fields, parameter domains, or unresolved subregions. They become boundary or interface terms only when a mathematically defined decomposition supplies a genuine boundary, induced geometry, and appropriate matching conditions. This preserves the MES commitment that the unknown is neither invented nor discarded.

The embedding relation is the full Gauss–Codazzi–Ricci compatibility system appropriate to the declared codimension. A scalar contraction alone is insufficient. The characteristic functional is selected according to the fixed dimension and topology of the GTCN; in even dimensions the Euler form and its boundary transgression provide one canonical option. A topological invariant is not assumed to classify every channel evidence vector one-to-one.

### 9.6 Five-Channel Evidence-State Vector

The Type-3 research status is recorded as

$$
\mathbf s=(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4,s_5),\qquad s_i\in\{A,R,U\}. \quad (55)

Here $A$ denotes support within a declared model domain, $R$ denotes exclusion within that same domain, and $U$ denotes an unresolved status. This representation avoids the overlap among the former four global categories. It also makes the scope of a negative result explicit: excluding all five specified channels excludes those channels within their examined domains, but does not establish universal physical impossibility unless completeness of the mechanism set is independently proved.

The GTCN can encode the channel-labelled evidence vector and investigate whether its components correspond to geometric or topological features. Such a correspondence is a substantive mathematical result to be established through the maps $\Psi_i$ and the characteristic structure, not an assumption used to define the vector.

**Level-appropriate claim.** The evidence-state vector is the bookkeeping structure for the five-channel research program. Type 3 is established only by a complete physically allowed path with calculated dynamics and independent target identification.

## Appendix A. Open Problems and Required Work

### A.1 Consistency and Model Existence

The consistency of A1–A6 and B1 has not been established. A formal analysis must specify the mathematical language, domains, and assumptions of the axiom system. If a model is constructed, its existence and properties must be demonstrated rather than inferred from the absence of an identified contradiction.

### A.2 Definition of the GTCN Characteristic Structure

The GTCN requires a fixed dimension, metric, connection, embedding, and characteristic structure. For even-dimensional compact oriented cases, the Euler form and boundary transgression provide a canonical Gauss–Bonnet–Chern construction. For other dimensions or topologies, the relevant characteristic class or network invariant must be specified. Its invariance, normalization, and behavior under channel-parameter changes must be proved. It must not be identified with a generic scalar-curvature integral without the required hypotheses.

### A.3 Construction of the GTCN and Nuclear Representation Maps

The embedding $\iota:\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathrm{web}}$ and the channel maps $\Psi_i$ must be constructed with explicit domains, codomains, regularity, and transformation properties. A representation map from geometric operators to the nuclear channel Hilbert space must preserve the relevant algebraic relations and specify its action on internal states, particle-number sectors, and continuum channels.

### A.4 Woods-Saxon and Shell-Model Validation

A reproducible nuclear calculation must specify the potential parameters, radial boundary conditions, effective interaction, model-space truncation, and numerical convergence criteria. The calculation must be benchmarked against appropriate evaluated nuclear data—for $^{197}\mathrm{Au}$, the $3/2^+$ ground state and the 409-keV $11/2^-$ isomer—before its matrix elements are used in the proposed bridge. Channel C1 (deformation) requires a full Nilsson-map scan over the $Z = 70$–$82$, $N = 100$–$126$ region.

### A.5 Environmental Decoherence

The rate $\gamma_{\mathrm{env}}$ for the relevant nuclear and atomic configuration must be calculated from a specified environment model. The calculation must distinguish nuclear decay widths, continuum-coupling widths, atomic or electronic interactions, thermal effects, and measurement-induced decoherence. Channel C5 (environment) is the direct computation of $\gamma_{\mathrm{env}}^{\text{bound}}(T)$, including internal-conversion, radiative, and phonon contributions. It is the single free parameter that spans six orders of magnitude in its effect on the drive gain, and its computation is the highest priority among the open problems.

### A.6 Exceptional-Point Existence and Robustness

The effective Hamiltonian family must be defined, and its EPs must be located through a controlled parameter search. Because a generic EP has codimension 2, the search must span at least two real parameters unless a protecting symmetry is specified. The calculation must demonstrate eigenvalue and eigenvector coalescence, quantify conditioning, and test robustness against perturbations and numerical resolution. Channels C2 (rotation) and C3 (strong field) both require this search, with C2 providing the pre-mixing that lowers C3's field threshold.

### A.7 Nonlinear Stability

The saturation functional $\mathcal N_{\mathrm{sat}}$ must be explicitly specified. Stationary solutions, bifurcation thresholds, local stability, and—if claimed—global attraction must be analyzed under stated boundary and normalization conditions.

### A.8 Non-Emptiness of the Dynamic Window

If a Zeno-type constraint and an adiabatic or Landau–Zener-type bound are used, their assumptions and parameter definitions must be stated. A Landau–Zener estimate depends on the minimum gap, the rate of parameter variation, and the normalization of the coupling. A nonempty operating window can be claimed only after the inequalities are written in a common set of units and shown to be jointly satisfiable for the specified model. The two-sided bound of Eq. (27) in Section 4.3 is the form in which this requirement is stated: the lower bound is the Landau–Zener timescale, the upper bound is the environmental decoherence time, and the window is nonempty only when the first is smaller than the second. This is the mathematical content of the "neither too fast nor too slow" condition.

### A.9 Reaction-Channel Connectivity and Target-Set Invariance

The direct-sum channel space must be supplemented by physical inter-sector reaction operators. Candidate paths must satisfy conservation laws, energy thresholds, nonzero matrix elements, and dynamical probability or flux balance. The target set must be invariant or reachable under the specified controlled dynamics.

The density-operator generator, control channels, admissible controls, target observable, and barrier function must be defined. The forward-invariance argument must be proved under explicit regularity and feasibility assumptions, or tested numerically with convergence and robustness checks. The extension of classical control-barrier functions to quantum density-matrix dynamics remains an open control-theoretic problem.

### A.10 Meaning of Existence, and the Two Guiding Principles

The terms formal specification, mathematical model existence, and physical realization must remain distinct. A construction may be formally specified without a model having been shown to exist; a mathematical model may exist without corresponding to a physical process; and a physical claim requires empirical evidence. These distinctions govern the interpretation of every result in this paper.

The two guiding principles of Section 1.4 are the operational form of this distinction:

- **P1** forbids treating Type 1 or Type 2 as if it were Type 3.
- **P2** forbids treating the openness of Type 3 as if it refuted Type 1 or Type 2.

Both principles must be held simultaneously. Holding only P1 produces overclaiming; holding only P2 produces overrejection. Holding both produces the level-appropriate claim that this paper attempts to make, and the five-channel evidence-state vector structure of Type 3 (Section 9.6) is the concrete form of that claim.

### A.11 Ricci Flow and the Dynamics of the GTCN

The static curvature functional of Section 5.3.3 is a starting point, not the full theory. A dynamic extension is the Ricci flow of Eq. (53). The stationary points of this flow are candidate "balanced" configurations in which passage and bottleneck coexist without global disconnection. Whether the Ricci flow of the GTCN converges to such a stationary point, and whether that point corresponds to a "gold-reachable" topological class, is an open mathematical problem.

### A.12 Variational Control and Minimum Topological Retrofitting

Given a target topological class for the GTCN characteristic functional, the variational problem of Eq. (54) defines a minimum topological retrofitting of the network: the smallest change to the channel configuration that moves the network into the target class. This is the natural formalization of "which bottleneck should be opened first." Solving it requires the full dynamic theory of A.11, and is therefore an open problem. Only the static functional and the Gauss–Codazzi–Ricci compatibility system are used in this paper.
