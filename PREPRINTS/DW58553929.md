## Abstract

This paper is the sister paper to DW58553928, *Creating Gold: New Beginning for Creating Matter*. It retains the title and the Modified Einstein Spherical (MES) foundations of the pair, including the three Gold Truths as foundational postulates of the MES cosmology architecture and the curvature-dependent fields inherited from DW58553914. Its purpose is to turn the unresolved physical realization problem for Gold-197 into a coordinated AI-assisted research program.

The paper retains five research channels: deformation (C1), rotation and Coriolis mixing (C2), strong-field dressing (C3), particle-number-sector and reaction-network construction (C4), and environmental interaction/internal conversion (C5). These channels have different physical roles but are integrated within one GTCN and one reaction-channel state space. A direct-sum Hilbert space organizes nuclear species, internal states, and open reaction channels; nonzero off-diagonal interaction operators provide the physical bridges between sectors. The existence of a direct sum alone does not imply a reaction, so candidate paths are derived from conservation laws, thresholds, matrix elements, and coupled-channel dynamics.

The Bottleneck Loop is retained as a proposed new physical-cycle hypothesis: the effective behavior of one channel may depend on another, and the resulting feedback may produce a collective response not present in isolated-channel calculations. Its coupling edges are specified as testable hypotheses. AI is treated as a computational discovery and orchestration system, with the explicit research objective of processing data for thousands of nuclides in hours where data access and computational workload permit, then prioritizing full nuclear-structure, Floquet, open-channel, and reaction-network calculations.

The former four-state classification is replaced by the five-channel evidence-state vector $\mathbf s=(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4,s_5)$ with $s_i\in\{A,R,U\}$. The GTCN remains the same-dimensional, same-level geometric structure used in DW58553928; any dimension-raising extension must satisfy explicit embedding, projection, and characteristic-class conditions. The paper develops a path from direct-sum channel space to physical reaction networks and from Floquet quasienergy structure to continuum-coupled particle emission and adaptive target-state control. A methodological analogy drawn from the Zhuangzi's parable of Wheelwright Pian clarifies why static open-loop prescriptions cannot replace responsive closed-loop control over dynamic nuclear coupling. No completed gold-transmutation experiment or numerical channel verdict is claimed; the paper specifies the calculations and independent tests required to decide them.

## 3. Five Research Channels and Their Physical Operators

The five channels are retained as a unified research architecture. They are not identical kinds of mechanism: C1–C3 are candidate activation mechanisms, C4 constructs reaction-sector connectivity, and C5 characterizes environmental and decay processes. Their roles are complementary and are evaluated jointly through the GTCN and the reaction-channel state space.

### 3.1 Channel C1: Deformation-Driven Threshold and Resonance Search

Let $H_{\mathrm{nuc}}(\beta_2,\gamma)$ be a specified nuclear Hamiltonian, including the central, spin-orbit, Coulomb, and residual-interaction terms. The relevant proton single-particle or many-body resonance is determined relative to the proton-emission threshold. Define the energy relative to that threshold by

$$
\epsilon_{\alpha}(\beta_2,\gamma) = E_{\alpha}(\beta_2,\gamma)-E_{\mathrm{thr}}, \quad (8)
$$

where the convention is that $\epsilon<0$ is below threshold and $\epsilon>0$ is above threshold. A threshold crossing is therefore

$$
\operatorname{Re}\epsilon_{\alpha}(\beta_2^*,\gamma^*)=0, \quad (9)
$$

together with a physically meaningful pole and a nonzero width in the open channel. This avoids equating a single-particle energy to a separation energy without specifying the energy zero and sign convention.

The calculation must report the Nilsson or deformed Woods–Saxon spectrum, the deformation domain, the proton threshold, resonance-pole trajectories, widths, and model uncertainties. The previously quoted estimate $\Delta\beta_2\sim3$–$5$ is treated as a preliminary model-dependent estimate, not a verified result, until a reproducible parameter scan and validity analysis are supplied.

**AI task.** Run a reproducible chart-wide scan, identify nuclei and substates close to threshold, and prioritize candidates for full calculation. A threshold crossing is not by itself proof of a viable gold-production path.

### 3.2 Channel C2: Rotational Coriolis Mixing

In a rotating frame, a standard starting Hamiltonian is

$$
H_{\mathrm{rot}}=H_{\mathrm{Nilsson}}-\omega J_x. \quad (10)
$$

The Coriolis term can mix configurations and break time-reversal symmetry for a fixed nonzero rotational frequency. This does not, by itself, prove nonzero Berry curvature, topological winding, continuum coupling, or an exceptional point. Berry curvature must be calculated from parameter-dependent eigenstates over a specified parameter manifold; an EP requires eigenvalue and eigenvector coalescence of a non-Hermitian operator family.

The calculation must specify the spin and rotational band, the accessible frequency range, Coriolis matrix elements, continuum-channel couplings, and induced widths. The estimate $\omega\sim0.1\,\mathrm{MeV}/\hbar$ is a candidate scale to investigate, not evidence that the required state and coupling are available in $^{197}\mathrm{Au}$.

**AI task.** Diagonalize the cranked Hamiltonian across the stated parameter domain, calculate configuration mixing and continuum matrix elements, and independently verify any Berry curvature or EP claim.

### 3.3 Channel C3: Strong-Field Dressing and Floquet Coupling

For a periodic drive,

$$
H(t)=H_0+V(t),\qquad V(t+T)=V(t),\qquad \omega=2\pi/T, \quad (11)
$$

the Floquet operator is

$$
H_F=H(t)-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}. \quad (12)
$$

The nuclear interaction must be derived from the relevant electromagnetic multipole coupling. For an electric quadrupole interaction, a schematic form is

$$
V_{E2}(t)=-\frac{1}{6}\sum_{ij}Q_{ij}\,\partial_i E_j(\mathbf r,t), \quad (13)
$$

with convention-dependent tensor normalization. Minimal coupling alone does not specify the nuclear quadrupole matrix element. The calculation must include field geometry, gradients, polarization, selection rules, radial and angular matrix elements, Floquet sidebands, and coupling to the appropriate particle-emission continuum.

A quasienergy crossing or avoided crossing does not itself imply nucleon emission. The relevant quantity is the partial width or transition rate into a specified open channel. If $\Gamma_p$ is an energy width, the corresponding rate is $\gamma_p=\Gamma_p/\hbar$. Rate comparisons must be made with rates, and width comparisons with widths.

For a Floquet state $|\Phi_F\rangle$ coupled to a proton continuum $|\chi_{p,E}\rangle$, a lowest-order expression is

$$
\Gamma_p(E)=2\pi\left|\langle\chi_{p,E}|V_{QP}|\Phi_F\rangle\right|^2\rho_p(E), \quad (14)
$$

with Floquet sideband energy matching included in the full calculation. The expression is a starting point, not a numerical prediction.

**AI task.** Perform Floquet parameter scans, surrogate-assisted inverse optimization, structured-field mode search, and independent full-solver verification. Any field estimate such as $10^{15}\,\mathrm{V/cm}$ must be traced to a defined Hamiltonian, geometry, and matrix element before being treated as a quantitative threshold.

### 3.4 Channel C4: Particle-Number Sectors and Reaction-Network Construction

C4 is the bridge from state-space organization to actual nuclear reactions. Let $\alpha$ label a complete reaction channel, including nuclear species $(A,Z)$, internal states, relative-motion quantum numbers, and emitted or absorbed particles. The reaction-channel Hilbert space is

$$
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{ch}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathcal C}\mathcal H_\alpha. \quad (15)
$$

The channel Hamiltonian has off-diagonal interaction blocks

$$
H_{\mathrm{ch}}=\bigoplus_\alpha H_{\alpha\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}V_{\alpha\beta},\qquad V_{\alpha\beta}=P_\alpha H P_\beta. \quad (16)
$$

The direct sum provides a common state space; the nonzero off-diagonal operators provide the physical bridges. If all relevant $V_{\alpha\beta}$ vanish, or if a transition is forbidden by conservation laws or thresholds, no physical transition follows from the direct-sum construction alone.

A candidate path $\alpha_0\to\alpha_1\to\cdots\to\alpha_f$ must satisfy, for the complete system, charge and baryon-number conservation, energy conservation, angular-momentum and parity constraints, nonzero reaction matrix elements, open-channel conditions, and adequate transition probabilities. The path may involve capture, transfer, emission, decay, or multi-step reactions; virtual intermediate sectors require a well-defined effective interaction and cannot be treated as real intermediate nuclei.

**AI task.** Extract evaluated masses, levels, separation energies, decay branches, and reaction data; generate candidate paths; calculate missing matrix elements with nuclear models; rank paths with uncertainty-aware scores. The AI objective is to process thousands of nuclides in hours for data extraction and preliminary graph search when data access and computational resources permit. High-precision reaction calculations remain separate, model-dependent tasks.

### 3.5 Channel C5: Environment, Internal Conversion, and Open-System Dynamics

Internal conversion is a nuclear de-excitation process in which nuclear transition energy is transferred to an atomic electron, which may be ejected. It is not automatically a decoherence bath that freezes a rotating state. Its effects must be separated into population decay, energy dissipation, phase decoherence, and possible measurement-induced dynamics.

A general open-system model may be written

$$
\frac{d\rho}{dt}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H,\rho]+\sum_\mu\left(L_\mu\rho L_\mu^\dagger-\frac12\{L_\mu^\dagger L_\mu,\rho\}\right), \quad (17)
$$

where the jump operators $L_\mu$ represent specified channels such as internal conversion, radiative decay, electron scattering, or coupling to material degrees of freedom. Each contribution must be derived from its coupling and density of states. If widths $\Gamma_\mu$ are used, the associated rates are $\gamma_\mu=\Gamma_\mu/\hbar$.

**AI task.** Compute internal-conversion coefficients and nuclear transition strengths; build the atomic-nuclear-environment coupling model; distinguish $T_1$ population lifetime from $T_2$ coherence time; and evaluate whether environmental processes suppress or assist the relevant channel dynamics. No stabilization or freezing effect is assumed in advance.

## 4. Four Classes of Verification Obligation

The five research channels generate several mathematical forms of verification. These four classes describe the shape of the proof or calculation required; they are not four mutually exclusive physical feasibility states.

### 4.1 Existential Obligations

C1 and C4 include existential questions: whether a deformation-dependent resonance exists, and whether a physically admissible path exists in the reaction-channel network. A positive result requires an explicit witness—parameter values, states, nonzero couplings, and a valid path. A negative result requires a controlled search or theorem over a declared domain.

### 4.2 Growth-Rate and Response Obligations

C2 requires the response of mixing amplitudes and continuum couplings as functions of rotational frequency and other parameters. The relevant output is not simply whether time-reversal symmetry is broken, but the computed dependence of matrix elements, widths, and state populations.

### 4.3 Supremum and Optimization Obligations

C3 requires optimization over field strength, frequency, polarization, geometry, and Floquet sidebands. A positive result identifies a parameter set with a specified partial width or rate. A negative result requires a rigorous upper bound over the declared admissible domain, including model error and numerical uncertainty.

### 4.4 Numerical-Value and Open-System Obligations

C5 requires numerical evaluation of decay widths, decoherence rates, and coherence times. Widths and rates must not be mixed: $\gamma_\mu=\Gamma_\mu/\hbar$. Internal conversion, radiative decay, and other processes contribute to different entries of the open-system generator and need not all represent pure dephasing.

### 4.5 Relationship Between Verification and Evidence States

The four verification classes are methods of establishing evidence. The final record is the five-channel evidence-state vector, not a four-state feasibility lattice. A numerical result may support one channel while leaving the complete reaction pathway unresolved. A global Type-3 conclusion requires the end-to-end path and target verification described in Sections 3 and 7.

## 5. AI-Assisted Discovery, Computation, and Adaptive Research

AI is not restricted to a clerical role. Within this program it can serve as a data-engineering system, model orchestrator, inverse optimizer, reaction-path discovery engine, uncertainty analyst, and feedback-control designer. The objective is to use AI to expand the reachable scale of nuclear-theory investigation while retaining auditable physical calculations.

### 5.1 Large-Scale Nuclide Data Processing

An AI-assisted pipeline can ingest evaluated nuclear databases and literature-derived records for thousands of nuclides, normalize identifiers and units, reconcile duplicate or conflicting entries, and build a queryable graph of masses, levels, separation energies, spins, parities, decay modes, and reaction data. Database-scale extraction and preliminary filtering can plausibly be organized for completion within hours, depending on access, data formats, and compute resources.

The pipeline must attach provenance and uncertainty to every datum. Missing values must remain missing rather than be imputed as physical facts. Quantities absent from evaluated databases require dedicated nuclear-structure or reaction calculations. Thus the research target is not merely "AI reads thousands of nuclei," but "AI produces a reproducible, provenance-aware nuclear reaction knowledge graph that can drive downstream calculations."

### 5.2 Inverse Optimization of Resonant Enhancement

The forward problem is to calculate the induced partial width or transition rate for a given field and nuclear state. The inverse problem is to find control parameters that optimize a specified observable subject to energy, field, and stability constraints. AI can train surrogate models on full Floquet calculations, identify candidate resonant regions, and propose parameter sets. Every proposed optimum must be re-evaluated by the full solver, with surrogate error and extrapolation uncertainty reported.

### 5.3 Structured-Mode Matching

AI can search structured electromagnetic modes whose spatial gradients and polarization maximize overlap with the actual nuclear multipole transition density. A mode-matching claim must use the full interaction Hamiltonian and the nuclear transition matrix element; matching a field pattern to a spherical harmonic such as $Y_{20}$ is not by itself evidence of enhanced nuclear coupling. Maxwell constraints, field gradients, selection rules, mode normalization, and energy delivery must be included.

### 5.4 Decoherence-Window and Lifetime Matching

For a driven transition, the relevant comparison is between the transition time, coherence time, population lifetime, and competing decay times. If $\Gamma$ is an energy width and $T_2$ is a coherence time, the dimensionless product $\Gamma T_2/\hbar$ may be used as a rough timescale indicator. It is not a sufficient activation criterion: the master equation, initial state, target population, and competing jump processes must also be solved.

### 5.5 Cross-Scale Analogical Transfer

AI can search for ideas in atomic physics, quantum optics, and condensed matter—dark states, parametric amplification, Floquet engineering, and geometric control—and translate each candidate into an explicit nuclear-scale Hamiltonian term. An analogy becomes a physical proposal only after dimensions, symmetries, conservation laws, selection rules, coupling strengths, and parameter domains have been derived.

### 5.6 Formal Bounds and Independent Verification

AI can assist in conjecture generation, symbolic derivation, numerical exploration, and proof checking. A formal upper bound is accepted only when its assumptions, domain, boundary conditions, approximation errors, and proof certificate are independently auditable. AI output is not itself a proof. Where a numerical surrogate is used, the final verdict must be checked against the full physical solver and independent code or method where feasible.

### 5.7 Adaptive Control of the Target-State Population

Let $\boldsymbol\lambda(t)$ denote controllable field parameters and let $\mathbf x(t)$ denote the populations or coherences of selected reaction channels. A feedback law may be written abstractly as

$$
\dot{\boldsymbol\lambda}=\mathcal F(\boldsymbol\lambda,\mathbf x,\mathbf x_{\mathrm{target}}), \quad (18)
$$

where $\mathcal F$ must be implemented by a physically realizable measurement-and-control protocol. ## 6. The Bottleneck Loop: A Testable Physical-Cycle Hypothesis

The Bottleneck Loop is retained as a central new physical idea of the five-channel architecture. It proposes that the bottleneck of one channel may be altered by another channel, and that these dependencies may form a closed cycle. This is stronger than saying that the channels are merely correlated: it proposes directional physical feedback with calculable consequences.

### 6.1 The Proposed Directed Coupling Network

The candidate network is represented by the directed dependencies C2→C3, C3→C5, C5→C2, C4→C1, and C1→C4; C3→C4 is an additional pathway to test when driven-state population changes subsequent reaction branching. This gives two candidate closed cycles, C2→C3→C5→C2 and C1↔C4. The network may contain further cycles, and its final topology is determined by the derived nonzero couplings rather than fixed by a diagram alone.

### 6.2 Physical Meaning of the Candidate Edges

- **C2 → C3:** Coriolis mixing may alter the state composition and multipole matrix elements entering the Floquet-driven continuum coupling. Compute the change in the relevant $V_{QP}$ and partial width with and without rotation.
- **C4 → C1:** A physically realized reaction path may enter a neighboring nuclear sector with a different deformation landscape and different resonance thresholds.
- **C5 → C2:** Environmental coupling may change rotational coherence, lifetime, and state populations. It may enhance a desired transfer in a restricted regime or suppress it through decoherence and decay; the sign is a calculation, not an assumption.
- **C1 → C4:** Deformation-dependent structure may change reaction matrix elements, level density, or branching ratios that determine which inter-sector edges are accessible.
- **C3 → C4:** Field-dressed states may alter reaction branching or populate intermediate states that open a subsequent reaction channel. This requires a joint driven-reaction calculation.
- **C3 → C5:** Driven-state population may change the available internal-conversion and radiative branches, thereby feeding back into the environmental dynamics.

Each edge must be assigned a coupling operator, initial and final states, control parameters, conservation rules, predicted effect, uncertainty, and a falsification condition. A complete loop requires a consistent set of edge maps whose composition produces a measurable change in a system-level observable. The proposed C2–C3–C5–C2 and C1–C4–C1 cycles are hypotheses until each edge and the composed response are calculated.

### 6.3 Loop Gain and Synergy

A loop gain can be defined only after a common observable and normalization are fixed. For example, if $Y$ is the target-state yield under a fixed initial condition and resource budget, a candidate synergy measure is

$$
S_Y=\frac{Y_{\mathrm{combined}}}{\max_i Y_i}, \quad (19)
$$

where $Y_{\mathrm{combined}}$ and $Y_i$ are computed under the same constraints. $S_Y>1$ indicates enhancement of target yield relative to the best isolated channel under that comparison protocol. It does not establish a universal synergy or a physical effect until the coupled dynamics and uncertainties are evaluated.

The concrete form of this metric, together with its comparison protocol, is given in the sister paper DW58553928, Section 6.7.4. The two papers use the same normalization convention: if $\Gamma$ denotes an energy width, all terms in a ratio must have energy units; if it denotes a rate, all terms must have inverse-time units.

### 6.4 Why the Loop Matters

The loop offers a new way to search for mechanisms that are invisible in isolated-channel analysis. AI can identify candidate feedback edges from large parameter scans, but physics determines whether those edges exist. A negative edge result is informative: it removes one proposed feedback mechanism while leaving the remaining network available for investigation.

The GTCN provides the common geometric representation of the loop. Its nodes, edges, cycles, curvature data, and embedding maps must be built from the corresponding physical operators and channel data. The loop is therefore a testable physical-cycle hypothesis, not merely a metaphor or an assumed enhancement.

## 7. Five-Channel Evidence-State Vector and Type-3 Decision

The former four-state classification is replaced by a five-channel evidence-state vector:

$$
\mathbf s=(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4,s_5),\qquad s_i\in\{A,R,U\}. \quad (20)
$$

Here $A$ means supported within a declared model and parameter domain; $R$ means ruled out within that same domain; and $U$ means unresolved or insufficiently constrained. The vector has $3^5$ formal combinations and preserves the independent status of each channel.

### 7.1 Why a Vector Is Preferable to Four Global States

The former categories overlapped: "at least one channel activated" could include both mixed and globally supported cases. The vector avoids this logical ambiguity and preserves evidence granularity. It also prevents a local channel result from being mistaken for an end-to-end physical result.

### 7.2 Type-3 Feasibility Requires an End-to-End Path

A Type-3 positive conclusion requires more than one channel marked $A$. It requires a connected sequence of physically allowed reaction edges from the initial state to the target state; conservation laws and thresholds must hold; all required matrix elements and branching probabilities must be quantified; the target-state population must be sufficient under a stated criterion; and the final nuclide must be independently identified.

If all five specified channels are marked $R$, the valid conclusion is that these five channels are ruled out within their examined model domains. It does not establish universal physical impossibility unless the completeness of the candidate mechanism set is separately proved. An unresolved component remains $U$ and must not be silently converted to either $A$ or $R$.

### 7.3 Negative Results as Research Progress

A negative verdict for one channel closes that candidate within its stated domain and reallocates effort to the remaining channels. A positive verdict identifies a supported component but does not alone prove the complete target pathway. The evidence vector therefore supports cumulative and auditable progress without collapsing distinct physical questions.

### 7.4 Relation to the GTCN

The GTCN can encode the five channel states as labelled geometric data and may reveal topological or geometric organization among different evidence vectors. A one-to-one correspondence between evidence vectors and topological classes is not assumed; it must be established by explicit maps and invariant calculations. The vector is the evidence record; the GTCN is the geometric structure in which its relationships are studied.

## 8. The Five Channels in the GTCN

The GTCN is the same-dimensional, same-level geometric child used in DW58553928. The five research channels are represented within it by explicit maps from physical parameter spaces and reaction-channel data to geometric structures.

### 8.1 Channel Maps and Geometric Data

For each channel $C_i$, define a parameter/state domain $\mathcal P_i$ and a map

$$
\Psi_i:\mathcal P_i\longrightarrow\mathfrak G(\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}),\qquad i=1,\ldots,5. \quad (21)
$$

The map must state what geometric object is produced: a metric deformation, connection change, curvature contribution, channel edge, boundary/interface datum, or open-system field. These possibilities are not interchangeable. A channel is not assigned a scalar-curvature source without deriving the map and its transformation properties.

### 8.2 Fixed Four-Dimensional Structure and Permitted Upgrades

The dimension $d_{\mathrm{GTC}} = 4$ and the codimension-one embedding conventions (for local ambient dimension $D_{\mathrm{MES}} = 5$) are inherited unchanged from DW58553928 (§1.6 and §5.3). Same-dimensional upgrades may enrich geometric fields, connection torsion, or network connectivity on the 4-manifold. A higher-dimensional extension ($d > 4$) must define the added coordinates, their physical or mathematical meaning, the projection recovering the four-dimensional GTCN, and compatibility of the embedding and characteristic forms. Both sister papers share the exact same $d_{\mathrm{GTC}} = 4$ specification.

### 8.3 Four-Dimensional Characteristic Functional and Unresolved Data

With $d_{\mathrm{GTC}} = 4$ ($m=2$), the Euler characteristic of a compact oriented GTCN 4-manifold is represented by the four-dimensional Gauss–Bonnet–Chern formula:

$$
\chi(\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}) = \int_{\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}}\mathcal E_{4}(g) + \int_{\partial\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}}\mathcal B_{3}(g,K), \quad (22)
$$

where $\mathcal E_4(g)$ is the normalized quadratic Euler curvature 4-form and $\mathcal B_3(g,K)$ is the three-dimensional boundary transgression form on $\partial\mathcal N_{\mathrm{GTC}}$. This is the same four-dimensional characteristic formula used in DW58553928, Section 5.3.3 (Eq. 39). A generic integral of scalar curvature plus a boundary curvature term is not topological in four dimensions.

Unresolved channels are retained as unknown fields or unresolved subdomains on the 4-manifold. They are represented by three-dimensional boundary/interface terms only when the geometry provides an actual hypersurface boundary, induced 3-metric, and Gauss–Codazzi matching conditions. Channel C4 is not automatically a boundary contribution: its reaction-sector extension is part of the four-dimensional network interior or an enlarged channel space unless a specific geometric decomposition proves otherwise.

### 8.4 GTCN and the Bottleneck Loop

The Bottleneck Loop appears in the GTCN as a directed cycle only when each proposed edge has a derived physical coupling and the composition is well-defined. The GTCN can then study cycle structure, geometric response, and characteristic invariants of the coupled network. This preserves the full theoretical role of GTCN while making its connection to nuclear dynamics explicit and falsifiable.

The lower bound is the adiabatic or Landau–Zener timescale; the upper bound is the environmental decoherence time.
