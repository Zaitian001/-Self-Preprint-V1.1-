Chirality as Curvature: Emergence from Attractor Basin Separatrix Asymmetry 2026-10-09

The origin of homochirality—the predominance of one enantiomeric form in biological systems—remains a fundamental problem in chemistry and the origin of life. Nonlinear effects in asymmetric synthesis and asymmetric autocatalysis demonstrate that small initial chiral imbalances can be amplified under suitable reaction conditions. However, the relationship between reaction kinetics, state-space geometry, and the selection of a particular handedness remains an open theoretical question.

Here we propose a geometric-dynamical architecture in which chiral selection is associated with the geometry of the separatrix between competing enantiomeric attractor basins. We represent the accessible chemical states by a statistical manifold equipped with a Fisher information metric and distinguish the metric from the nonequilibrium potential governing chemical dynamics. A consistently defined separatrix-curvature descriptor, denoted by $\Delta\kappa_{\mathcal S}$, is introduced to quantify a specified form of geometric asymmetry under a defined mirror correspondence.

We establish a conditional equivalence theorem: within a model class satisfying explicit structural assumptions, nonzero chiral-selection bias, nonzero separatrix-curvature asymmetry, and non-invariance of the Fisher metric under a mirror involution are equivalent. The proof is mathematically exact within these assumptions; the physical validity of the assumptions for particular chemical systems remains to be established independently.

We further develop two related hypotheses. First, topological descriptors of chemical state-space dynamics may help characterize the robustness of autocatalytic amplification, provided a nontrivial invariant and its relationship to measurable chemical observables can be established. Second, separatrix geometry may influence nonlinear amplification through its effect on transition actions and rates. A conditional stochastic model illustrates how an exponential dependence of transition rates can produce a hyperbolic-tangent response, while identifying the additional assumptions required for that result.

Four experimentally testable research directions are proposed: reconstructing dynamical state-space geometry, testing topological robustness, measuring geometric contributions to amplification thresholds, and distinguishing geometry-mediated effects of external fields from conventional chemical mechanisms.

This work is a companion to Inaugurating the Era of Super Cell: Curvature-Driven Geometric Synthetic Biology for Cell Fate Reprogramming (DW58553919). Together, the papers propose Geometric Selection Theory as a cross-scale research programme connecting molecular handedness and cellular fate selection through the geometry of competing dynamical basins. The present manuscript establishes a conditional mathematical framework and a set of falsifiable hypotheses, rather than claiming that a universal geometric mechanism of homochirality has already been demonstrated.

Its scientific value will depend on whether geometric descriptors improve prediction and intervention beyond suitable molecular, statistical, and dynamical baselines.
