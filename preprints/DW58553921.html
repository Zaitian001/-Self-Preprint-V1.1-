1. Introduction

1.1 The problem of unification

Contemporary physics describes different scales of nature through several highly successful but conceptually distinct architectures. General relativity describes gravitational geometry, the Standard Model describes known elementary particles and their interactions, quantum field theory provides the language of particle dynamics, and cosmology addresses the large-scale evolution of the universe.

These theories are empirically powerful, but several foundational questions remain open. They include the physical origin of particle mass, the relationship between spacetime geometry and quantum properties, the emergence of intrinsic angular momentum, the physical interpretation of entanglement, and the relationship between microscopic and cosmological structures.

The MES cosmology architecture approaches these questions from a geometric starting point. Rather than treating matter as a collection of entities placed on an independently prescribed background, it investigates whether stable matter-like structures can be understood as emergent configurations of a dynamical geometric system.

This is a proposed research program, not a claim that established theories have been displaced.

1.2 The central proposition: All Things as Wuxing Attractors

The central proposition of this paper is:

All Things within the physical domain, including but not limited to all particles, may be investigated as Wuxing Attractors embedded in the MES Universe Web.

Here, All Things is deliberately broader than the particle sector. The proposed scope includes, but is not limited to, elementary particles, composite particles, nuclei, atoms, fields, macroscopic matter, stars, black holes, cosmic rays, and large-scale cosmic structures.

This statement is a foundational hypothesis of the MES–Wuxing research program. It does not imply that every listed object has already been shown to correspond to a mathematically established Wuxing Attractor. Such an identification requires an explicit state space, a dynamical law, a stability criterion, and observable consequences for each relevant class of systems.

A Wuxing Attractor (WA) is defined here as a candidate persistent nonlinear state organized by five modes associated with $\mathbb{Z}_5$, with its dynamics governed by a restoring mechanism associated with ZQP.

1.3 The MES–Wuxing–ZQP architecture

The proposed architecture has four interconnected components:

  1. MES Universe Web: a closed spatial $S^3$ geometry equipped with a dynamical curvature field.
  2. Curvature knots: localized or extended geometric configurations proposed as the structural basis of matter-like entities.
  3. Wuxing Attractors: five-mode nonlinear state structures used to represent persistent and differentiated configurations.
  4. Zaitian Quantum Power: a proposed universal nonlinear mechanism for restoring, selecting, or stabilizing admissible configurations.

The relationship can be summarized schematically as

$$ \text{MES geometry} \longrightarrow \text{ZQP dynamics} \longrightarrow \text{Wuxing Attractors} \longrightarrow \text{observable physical properties}. \quad (1) $$

Equation (1) is an architectural summary, not a derived dynamical equation.

1.4 Logical status of claims

To distinguish foundational assumptions from demonstrated results, this paper uses four categories:

  • Postulate: a foundational proposition adopted as part of the architecture.
  • Conditional mathematical result: a conclusion that follows if its stated assumptions hold.
  • Structured hypothesis: a proposed relationship whose complete derivation or empirical validation remains outstanding.
  • Open problem: a clearly identified task for future mathematical, numerical, or experimental work.

The distinction is especially important for the mass formula, variable-light-speed interpretation, quantum entanglement, superquantum extension, and proton spin.

3. The MES Universe Web

3.1 Closed spatial geometry

A representative homogeneous metric for a closed spatial geometry is

$$ ds^2=-c(R)^2\,dt^2+a(t)^2\,d\Omega_3^2. \quad (12) $$

Here, $a(t)$ is the scale factor, $d\Omega_3^2$ is the metric on a unit three-sphere, and $c(R)$ denotes the proposed curvature-dependent propagation speed.

Equation (12) is a model ansatz. If $c(R)$ varies in spacetime, a complete relativistic formulation must specify the metric convention, the role of the lapse function, the operational definition of measured light speed, and the relationship between coordinates and physical clocks.

The expression should not be interpreted as a derivation of variable light speed from the topology of $S^3$ alone.

3.2 A scalar curvature-field model

For a tractable mathematical investigation, define the dimensionless curvature variable

$$ \widetilde{R}=\frac{R}{R_0}. \quad (13) $$

Since the scalar curvature $R$ and reference curvature $R_0$ both have dimensions of inverse length squared, $\widetilde{R}$ is dimensionless.

A representative effective action is

$$ S[\widetilde{R}]=\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{A}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\widetilde{R}\,\partial_\nu\widetilde{R}-V(\widetilde{R})\right]. \quad (14) $$

The coefficient $A$ supplies the units required for the action under the chosen unit convention. The potential $V$ must have the corresponding action-density dimensions.

For example, an effective potential may be written as

$$ V(\widetilde{R})=\frac{A\mu^2}{2}\widetilde{R}^2+\frac{A\eta}{4}\widetilde{R}^4. \quad (15) $$

Here $\mu$ has dimensions of inverse length and $\eta$ has dimensions of inverse length squared in the displayed normalization. Other normalizations are possible, but they must be specified consistently.

Variation with respect to $\widetilde{R}$ yields a scalar-field equation of the form

$$ A\,\Box\widetilde{R}+\frac{dV}{d\widetilde{R}}=0. \quad (16) $$

For the potential in Equation (15), this becomes

$$ \Box\widetilde{R}+\mu^2\widetilde{R}+\eta\widetilde{R}^3=0. \quad (17) $$

The sign of the kinetic term and the sign convention for $\Box$ depend on the metric signature and action convention. Equation (17) is a representative scalar-field model, not the complete covariant field equation of the MES cosmology architecture.

The MES architecture may contain additional geometric terms. Those terms must be defined and coupled according to the architecture's own field equations. The present scalar model should not be confused with a complete derivation of the MES Universe Equation.

3.3 A phenomenological breathing mode

A small oscillatory perturbation of the scale factor may be represented as

$$ a(t)=a_0\left[1+\epsilon\cos(\omega_b t)\right],\qquad |\epsilon|\ll1. \quad (18) $$

This expression describes a phenomenological breathing mode. The associated scalar curvature cannot, in general, be specified by a universal proportionality to the scale factor without choosing the spacetime metric, curvature convention, and field equations.

Accordingly, the curvature oscillation must be calculated from the selected MES solution rather than assumed to follow from Equation (18) alone.

The MES scaling hypothesis adopted in this version is

$$ c(R)=c_0\left(\frac{R}{R_0}\right)^{1/2}. \quad (19) $$

The associated reduced Planck constant is proposed to scale as

$$ \hbar(R)=\hbar_0\left(\frac{R_0}{R}\right)^{1/2}. \quad (20) $$

The mass scale is proposed to obey

$$ m(R)=m_0\left(\frac{R_0}{R}\right)^{1/2}. \quad (21) $$

These relations require $R/R_0>0$ in the displayed real-valued form. If the intended curvature domain includes zero or negative scalar curvature, an extension of the scaling law must be specified.

Equations (19)–(21) are linked hypotheses within the MES architecture. They are not derived from the existence of a closed $S^3$ manifold alone.

4.2 Invariant products under the proposed scaling

Multiplying Equations (19) and (20) gives

$$ \hbar(R)\,c(R)=\hbar_0\,c_0. \quad (22) $$

Likewise, Equations (19) and (21) give

$$ m(R)\,c(R)=m_0\,c_0. \quad (23) $$

These two identities follow algebraically from the specified scaling relations. They do not establish that either $\hbar$ or $c$ varies in a physically measurable way.

A physical interpretation requires a complete theory of clocks, rulers, wave propagation, local measurements, and dimensionless observables. In particular, a variation of dimensional constants cannot be established solely by assigning position-dependent values to their numerical representations.

Equation (24) is a propagation-time expression under the assumed local-speed rule. It does not independently establish that a signal can exceed the locally measured light speed, violate a causal cone, or transmit information superluminally.

Those conclusions require the complete metric and causal structure of the MES model.

  • $L_0$ has dimensions of length;

Equation (30) is a structured hypothesis. It expresses a proposed relationship between mass, curvature, a characteristic length, and ZQP intervention depth. It is not a first-principles derivation of the observed particle spectrum.

The two remaining modes $|\nu_{\mathrm{金}}\rangle$ and $|\nu_{\mathrm{水}}\rangle$ are presently defined as geometric phase modes — internal degrees of freedom associated with the curvature field and ZQP coupling, rather than independent particle species. Their possible physical interpretation beyond the present model remains an open problem.

In particular, the fifth-mode construction must not be used to reintroduce a sterile neutrino under another name without specifying the relevant observable distinctions and experimental consequences.

8.3 Mass and coherence length

A candidate coherence-length definition is

$$ L_q(R)=\frac{\hbar(R)\,c(R)}{\Lambda_q\,|B_\nu^{(q)}|}. \quad (38) $$

If one defines the associated energy scale by

$$ E_q=\frac{\hbar(R)\,c(R)}{L_q(R)}, \quad (39) $$

then substituting Equation (38) yields $E_q=\Lambda_q|B_\nu^{(q)}|$. This is an algebraic consequence of the definition, not an independent derivation of a neutrino mass.

To identify $E_q$ with $m_{\nu_q}c^2$, the relevant energy–mass convention and the dynamical origin of the coherence length must be established.

8.4 Neutrino mass constraints

Neutrino masses are constrained by oscillation measurements, cosmological observations, and direct kinematic searches. A proposed MES spectrum must be compared with the relevant mass-squared differences and bounds, while distinguishing mass eigenstates from flavor eigenstates.

Any numerical spectrum adopted in a future version should be accompanied by current experimental sources, uncertainties, and a clear statement of which parameters are fitted and which are predicted.

9. Nucleon Geometry and the Emergence of Spin

9.2 Why topology alone does not prove proton spin

For an appropriately defined SU(3)-valued effective field $U:S^3\to SU(3)$, the homotopy relation

$$ \pi_3(SU(3))\cong\mathbb{Z} \quad (42) $$

permits integer-valued topological sectors.

With a suitable orientation and normalization, one may define a winding number

$$ B[U]=\frac{1}{24\pi^2}\int_{S^3}\mathrm{Tr}\,\bigl[(U^{-1}dU)^3\bigr]\in\mathbb{Z}. \quad (43) $$

The exact overall sign depends on conventions for the trace and orientation.

Equations (42) and (43) establish the mathematical possibility of classifying suitable field configurations by an integer topological charge. They do not establish that the proton spin equals one-half, and they do not establish that every SU(3) color-gauge configuration carries the physical baryon number.

The physical interpretation of $B[U]$ depends on the field theory in which $U$ is defined. In a chiral effective theory, topological winding can be related to baryon number under specific conditions. This relationship cannot be transferred automatically to an arbitrary MES geometric field.

9.3 ZQP-driven geometric spin emergence

Postulate 8 reverses the question. Rather than presupposing a proton spin of one-half and identifying it with a winding number, it asks whether ZQP-driven geometric dynamics can produce stable quantum states with the appropriate rotational transformation properties.

Let $\mathcal{C}_B$ denote a configuration space restricted to a well-defined topological sector, and let $\gamma_{2\pi}$ represent a path corresponding to a full spatial rotation of a candidate configuration. A complete quantization must determine whether this path is contractible and what phase the corresponding quantum state acquires.

If the model independently establishes

$$ \Psi[\gamma_{2\pi}(U)]=-\Psi[U], \quad (44) $$

then the state transforms with a minus sign under a $2\pi$ rotation. In an ordinary quantum theory with the appropriate rotation representation, this is consistent with half-integer spin.

However, Equation (44) is a condition to be derived from the model's configuration-space topology and quantization rules. It does not follow from the integer winding number alone.

9.4 The distinction between half-integer spin and spin one-half

The angular momentum algebra permits

$$ J\in\{\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{5}{2},\ldots\} \quad (45) $$

for half-integer-spin representations.

Consequently, even a successful derivation of the minus sign in Equation (44) would not by itself select $J=1/2$. To establish the proton's specific spin, the theory must additionally derive the relevant energy spectrum, quantum numbers, and state-selection mechanism.

10. Quantum Entanglement and the Geometric Correlation Hypothesis

This is a standard quantum-state form under the assumption that the two basis states represent the appropriate orthogonal degrees of freedom. The expression does not establish that the subsystems are physically connected by a single geometric knot.

10.2 Geometric hypothesis

The proposed MES interpretation is

$$ \text{entanglement}\rightsquigarrow\text{shared geometric correlation structure}. \quad (47) $$

The hypothesis is that the correlated subsystems may be represented as parts or endpoints of a common geometric structure. To turn this into a physical model, the relationship between the geometric state and the quantum density operator must be explicitly defined.

In particular, the model must reproduce quantum measurement probabilities and the correlations observed in Bell-type experiments.

10.3 A candidate geometric kernel

A possible positive elliptic operator on $S^3$ can be written as

$$ \hat{A}=-\Delta_{S^3}+\mu_K^2,\qquad \mu_K>0. \quad (48) $$

Here, $-\Delta_{S^3}$ is the nonnegative Laplace–Beltrami operator on the specified spatial geometry, and $\mu_K$ has dimensions of inverse length.

The corresponding Green operator is

$$ \hat{K}=\hat{A}^{-1}. \quad (49) $$

Let the eigenfunctions satisfy

$$ -\Delta_{S^3}\phi_k=\kappa_k^2\phi_k. \quad (50) $$

For an orthonormal basis under the selected volume measure,

$$ \int_{S^3}\phi_k(\mathbf{x})\,\phi_{k'}^*(\mathbf{x})\,dV=\delta_{kk'}. \quad (51) $$

The formal kernel is then

$$ K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}')=\sum_k\frac{\phi_k(\mathbf{x})\,\phi_k^*(\mathbf{x}')}{\kappa_k^2+\mu_K^2}. \quad (52) $$

The dimensions of the kernel depend on the normalization of the eigenfunctions and the measure. The kernel also depends on the chosen metric and boundary conditions.

Equations (48)–(52) define a possible mathematical tool for representing spatial correlations. They do not demonstrate that quantum entanglement is mediated by this kernel, nor that the kernel determines a quantum measurement outcome.

10.4 Nonlocal correlation and causality

The idea of "instantaneous activation" is retained only as a geometric hypothesis about nonlocal correlation. It is not asserted to be a physical signal traveling instantaneously between spatially separated systems.

A valid MES account must demonstrate that its predicted measurement statistics reproduce the relevant quantum correlations and satisfy the no-signaling condition. It must also specify how the model's causal structure is related to operationally measured propagation speeds.

10.5 Relation to Bell inequalities

For the standard CHSH experiment, the local-hidden-variable bound is $2$, while quantum theory predicts a maximum value of $2\sqrt{2}$ for the usual quantum-mechanical correlations under the standard assumptions.

A proposed MES extension must not conflate a mathematical parameter with an experimentally measured CHSH correlation. Any claimed departure from the standard quantum bound requires a complete specification of the observables, probabilities, normalization, and experimental protocol.

The superquantum expression is not counted as an additional foundational postulate in this version. The eight-postulate architecture is preserved, while the proposed extension is formulated separately as a conjectural research hypothesis.

11.1 The superquantum conjecture

Superquantum Conjecture (conjectural extension). The MES–ZQP architecture investigates whether curvature-dependent geometric dynamics can define an effective correlation structure that differs from standard quantum predictions under a precisely specified extension of the measurement model.

The candidate expression is

$$ S_{\mathrm{ZQP}}=2\sqrt{2}\left(1+\frac{\lambda_q R}{s_q R_0}\right). \quad (53) $$

Status of the superquantum expression. The expression $S_{\mathrm{ZQP}}=2\sqrt{2}(1+\lambda_q R/(s_q R_0))$ is presented only as a mathematical placeholder for a possible curvature-dependent correlation. It is not claimed that this expression is dimensionally identical to a CHSH parameter, nor that it violates the Tsirelson bound in any operational sense. A consistent derivation from the MES measurement model remains an open problem.

For the ratio in parentheses to be dimensionless, $\lambda_q$ and $s_q$ must have compatible dimensions, and $R/R_0$ is dimensionless. If $\lambda_q$ and $s_q$ are both dimensionless, the expression is dimensionally consistent.

11.2 Mathematical and empirical requirements

If Equation (53) is intended to represent an actual CHSH correlation parameter, it must be reconciled with the definitions of the measured observables and the probability structure of the theory.

In standard quantum theory, a CHSH parameter formed from the usual dichotomic observables is bounded by $2\sqrt{2}$. Therefore, if the correction factor in Equation (53) is positive and nonzero, the expression exceeds the standard quantum bound. Such a result cannot be presented as an ordinary quantum-mechanical prediction without further qualification.

A superquantum interpretation would require, at minimum:

  1. a precise definition of the proposed observables;
  2. a normalized probability model;
  3. explicit predictions for measurement outcomes;
  4. a demonstration of no-signaling, or a clearly stated alternative;
  5. a causal and relativistic interpretation;
  6. a testable distinction from standard quantum theory.

Open Problem 6. Derive or revise Equation (53) from a fully specified MES–ZQP measurement model, and determine whether the resulting theory is mathematically consistent, operationally meaningful, and empirically distinguishable from standard quantum mechanics.

These are propositions of the research program, not a list of experimentally established results.

14.2 What the architecture does not claim

This paper does not claim that:

  • the MES architecture has experimentally replaced general relativity or the Standard Model;
  • all physical entities have already been proven to be Wuxing Attractors;
  • the mass formula has been derived from first principles;
  • the curvature-dependent light-speed relation has been experimentally verified;
  • topology alone proves proton spin $1/2$;
  • the geometric entanglement hypothesis has been proven to reproduce all quantum measurement statistics;
  • the superquantum expression has been experimentally verified;
  • the sterile-neutrino hypothesis has been excluded from all possible physical theories.

The sterile-neutrino model choice in Section 8 applies to the proposed MES–Wuxing mode construction only.

14.3 The significance of the eighth postulate

The eighth postulate changes the direction of the spin problem. Instead of assuming that spin is a pre-existing quantum number and searching for a topological label, the MES–ZQP program asks whether geometric dynamics can generate stable states whose rotational transformation properties correspond to spin.

This is a more foundational question, but it also imposes a more demanding proof requirement. The model must derive the quantum state space, the rotational action, the angular-momentum algebra, and the particular representation associated with the proton.

ZQP can be proposed as a mechanism for selecting and stabilizing geometric modes. It cannot be identified with the origin of spin in a demonstrated sense until the required dynamics and quantization have been supplied.

14.4 The distinction between architecture and derivation

The MES architecture is the theoretical starting point of this paper. The purpose of the mathematical sections is not to presuppose that every relation has already been derived, but to identify the equations, definitions, and tests needed to make the architecture precise.

A complete future treatment should integrate the curvature field equations, ZQP dynamics, state-space structure, and observable predictions into a consistent mathematical model. It should also establish the assumptions under which the model reduces to established physical theories in their tested domains.

The mass formula remains a structured hypothesis. The geometric interpretation of entanglement remains a hypothesis requiring recovery of quantum statistics and no-signaling. The superquantum expression remains a conjectural extension. The proton-spin explanation remains an open proof program rather than an established theorem.

The central contribution of this paper is therefore the consolidation of a broad geometric research architecture with clearer proof obligations, corrected dimensional relations, and a new principle for the geometric-dynamical investigation of spin.
